================================================================================
HOW TO PREPARE FOR BEHAVIORAL INTERVIEWS
================================================================================

OVERVIEW
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Behavioral interviews are NOT something you can wing. The candidates who
perform best have systematically prepared a "story bank" — a curated set of
real experiences mapped to competency categories — and have practiced
delivering them out loud, under time pressure, until the answers feel
natural without being robotic.

This guide walks you through the full preparation process step by step.

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
STEP 1 — AUDIT YOUR CAREER FOR STORIES
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Start by reviewing your entire career history and extracting experiences that
demonstrate impact, challenge, or growth.

For each role you've held, answer these questions:
  • What was the hardest technical or non-technical problem I solved?
  • When did I have to lead or influence without authority?
  • What project am I most proud of and why?
  • When did I fail or make a significant mistake?
  • When did I disagree with my manager or a peer?
  • When did I have to manage competing priorities?
  • When did I go significantly above and beyond my job description?
  • What was the most ambiguous situation I navigated?
  • When did I have to deliver bad news to stakeholders?
  • Who did I mentor or help grow?

AIM FOR 10–15 HIGH-QUALITY STORIES TOTAL.

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
STEP 2 — BUILD YOUR STORY BANK
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
A story bank is a structured collection of your best experiences, each written
out in STAR format. Use the template below for each story.

STORY BANK TEMPLATE:
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
STORY TITLE:     [Short memorable name, e.g., "Payments Launch Rescue"]
ROLE/COMPANY:    [Your title, company name, approximate date]
CORE COMPETENCY: [Primary theme: Leadership / Conflict / Failure / etc.]
ALSO COVERS:     [Other competencies this story can address]

SITUATION:
  [2–4 sentences of context, stakes, and the triggering challenge]

TASK:
  [1–2 sentences on your specific ownership/responsibility]

ACTION:
  [5–8 bullet points of specific, active steps with reasoning]
  •
  •
  •
  •
  •

RESULT:
  [Quantified outcome + reflection/learning]

KEY METRICS TO MENTION:
  [List all numbers: %, $, users, time saved, etc.]

FOLLOW-UP ANSWERS:
  Q: What would you do differently?
  A: [Your honest reflection]
  Q: What was the most difficult part?
  A: [The real challenge]
  Q: How did others react?
  A: [Team/stakeholder response]
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
STEP 3 — MAP STORIES TO COMPETENCIES
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
One well-crafted story can answer MULTIPLE behavioral questions.
Map each story to the competency categories it covers.

Create a coverage matrix like this:

STORY NAME               | Lead | Conflict | Failure | Ambiguity | Ownership | Pressure
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Payments Launch Rescue   |  ✓   |          |         |    ✓      |     ✓     |    ✓
ML Engineer Offboarding  |  ✓   |    ✓     |         |    ✓      |     ✓     |
Data Pipeline Outage     |      |          |   ✓     |           |     ✓     |    ✓
Team Restructure         |  ✓   |    ✓     |   ✓     |           |           |
Feature Descoping        |      |    ✓     |         |    ✓      |           |    ✓
Mentor Story             |  ✓   |          |         |           |     ✓     |
...

GOAL: Have at least 1–2 stories for every major competency.
BONUS: Have "backup" stories in case the interviewer says "give me another."

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
STEP 4 — QUANTIFY EVERY RESULT
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Interviewers score quantified results significantly higher.
Even if you don't have exact numbers, make reasonable estimates and say so.

TURNING VAGUE RESULTS INTO STRONG RESULTS:

✗ Weak: "The feature was well-received."
✓ Strong: "We shipped to 120K users in the first week and achieved a 4.7/5
           rating in in-app feedback. DAU increased 18% within 30 days."

✗ Weak: "I improved the process."
✓ Strong: "I reduced the manual review process from 4 hours to 45 minutes per
           week — saving the team roughly 130 hours per quarter."

✗ Weak: "The team appreciated my leadership."
✓ Strong: "Two engineers on my team cited this project as their reason for
           staying through a difficult company period. Attrition in our team
           was 0% that year vs 22% company-wide."

METRICS TO ALWAYS SEEK OUT:
  • Revenue impact ($, %)
  • User/customer impact (# affected, satisfaction %)
  • Engineering metrics (latency ↓, error rate ↓, uptime ↑)
  • Time saved (hours/week, sprint velocity %)
  • Team metrics (attrition, CSAT, engagement scores)
  • Business metrics (conversion %, churn %, LTV)

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
STEP 5 — RESEARCH THE COMPANY'S VALUES FRAMEWORK
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Each company has a specific framework they use to evaluate behavioral fit.
Research it BEFORE your interview and map your stories to their framework.

AMAZON → 16 Leadership Principles (LP) — see 04_Company_Frameworks/00_Amazon_Leadership_Principles.txt
  • Match each story to 1–2 LPs
  • Amazon interviewers almost always anchor on specific LPs

GOOGLE → "Googleyness" + 4 hiring attributes — see 01_Google_Googleyness_and_Leadership.txt
  • Cognitive ability, Leadership, Googleyness, Role-related knowledge

META → Meta Core Values — see 02_Meta_Core_Values.txt
  • Move fast, Focus on long-term impact, Build together

MICROSOFT → Growth Mindset + GRIT + Collaboration
APPLE → Quality, Craftsmanship, "Is this the best we can do?"

Do this research 24–48 hours before each interview.

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
STEP 6 — PRACTICE OUT LOUD
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Reading your stories silently is NOT enough. The only way to truly prepare is
to speak them out loud.

PRACTICE METHODS (in order of effectiveness):

1. MOCK INTERVIEWS WITH A PARTNER
   • Ask a friend, colleague, or coach to play interviewer
   • Practice full questions: question → answer → 2–3 follow-ups
   • Ask for honest feedback on clarity, conciseness, and confidence

2. RECORD YOURSELF
   • Use your phone or laptop camera
   • Watch it back — painful but invaluable
   • Check: body language, filler words, pacing, eye contact

3. TALK TO A MIRROR
   • Good for warming up before the real thing
   • Less effective than recording but better than silent rehearsal

4. TIMED PRACTICE
   • Set a 3-minute timer and deliver your answer
   • Get used to fitting the key components within the limit

5. INTERLEAVED PRACTICE
   • Don't just practice one story repeatedly
   • Mix stories: practice "leadership" then "failure" then "conflict"
   • Builds retrieval flexibility for the real interview

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
STEP 7 — PREPARE YOUR "TELL ME ABOUT YOURSELF" ANSWER
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
This is almost always the first question. Have a tight 90-second version ready.

FORMULA:
  [Current role + key responsibility]
  + [Major career highlight or expertise]
  + [What you're excited about in this role/company]

EXAMPLE:
  "I'm a senior software engineer with 7 years of experience, currently at
   Acme Corp where I lead the payments infrastructure team — a 6-person team
   responsible for processing $2B in annual transaction volume.

   My background is in distributed systems and reliability engineering. One of
   my proudest achievements was redesigning our payment settlement system,
   which improved transaction success rate from 94% to 99.3% and added an
   estimated $8M in annual recovered revenue.

   I'm really drawn to this role at [Company] because of your focus on [X].
   I'm eager to bring my systems expertise to a product used by [Y users] at
   this scale."

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
STEP 8 — PREPARE YOUR QUESTIONS FOR THE INTERVIEWER
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Always have 3–5 thoughtful questions ready. This signals genuine interest and
gives you valuable information to evaluate the role.

GREAT QUESTION CATEGORIES:
  Team & Culture:   "How does the team handle technical disagreements?"
  Role Clarity:     "What does success look like in the first 90 days?"
  Growth:           "What's the typical career trajectory for this role?"
  Challenges:       "What's the biggest unsolved challenge for the team right now?"
  Interviewer Perspective: "What do you enjoy most about working here?"

AVOID:
  • Questions easily answered on the website
  • Questions about vacation/benefits (save for HR/recruiter)
  • Overly generic questions ("What's the culture like?")

Full guide in: 03_Common_Behavioral_Questions/07_Questions_to_Ask_Interviewer.txt

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
STEP 9 — THE WEEK-BEFORE PREPARATION PLAN
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
7 days out:
  □ Finalize story bank (10–15 stories, all in STAR format)
  □ Map stories to company's values framework
  □ Complete at least one timed practice of each story

4–5 days out:
  □ Do 2 full mock interviews with a partner
  □ Refine weak stories based on feedback
  □ Research the specific team, interviewer (LinkedIn), and product

2–3 days out:
  □ Record yourself on video for 3–4 stories
  □ Finalize your "Tell Me About Yourself" answer
  □ Prepare your 5 questions for the interviewer

1 day out:
  □ Light review — don't over-cram
  □ Read through your story bank titles and key metrics
  □ Confirm logistics (time, platform, interviewer name)
  □ Get 8 hours of sleep

Day of interview:
  □ Warm up by speaking 2–3 stories out loud in the morning
  □ Have your story bank notes nearby (for virtual interviews)
  □ Test your audio/video 30 minutes before
  □ Enter the interview calm, prepared, and curious

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
STEP 10 — AFTER THE INTERVIEW
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  • Send a thank-you email within 24 hours (see 04_Company_Frameworks/06_Handling_Rejection_and_Follow_Up.txt)
  • Write down every question you were asked while they're fresh
  • Note which answers felt strong and which felt weak
  • Refine your story bank based on gaps you discovered
  • Treat every interview as training data for the next one

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
STORY BANK STARTER — 15 STORY IDEAS
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
If you're not sure where to start, these 15 story types cover all major
behavioral competencies:

1.  A project you led from ambiguity to delivery
2.  A time you disagreed with your manager and how it resolved
3.  A significant technical or product failure you owned
4.  A time you mentored someone through a difficult situation
5.  A tight deadline you met through creative problem-solving
6.  A conflict between two team members you helped resolve
7.  A time you influenced a major decision without formal authority
8.  A time you had to say no to a stakeholder's request
9.  A time you went above and beyond your job description
10. A time you had to change your approach mid-project
11. A time you made a decision with incomplete data
12. A time you had to deliver difficult feedback
13. A time you advocated for a user/customer over business pressure
14. A time you improved a process that made your team more effective
15. A time you took on a stretch assignment outside your comfort zone
