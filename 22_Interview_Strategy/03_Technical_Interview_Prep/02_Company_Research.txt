================================================================================
COMPANY RESEARCH: HOW TO PREPARE FOR A SPECIFIC COMPANY
================================================================================
Purpose: Generic interview prep is necessary but not sufficient. Company-specific
         research is what separates candidates who clear every loop from those
         who fail at the culture/fit stage or misjudge the bar.
================================================================================

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. WHY COMPANY RESEARCH MATTERS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Research serves four goals:

  1. CALIBRATE THE BAR
     Different companies ask different difficulty levels and focus areas.
     Google loves hard algorithm problems. Amazon emphasizes LP/behavioral.
     Meta values product thinking. Stripe asks about APIs and distributed systems.

  2. SIGNAL GENUINE INTEREST
     "Why this company?" is asked in virtually every interview.
     A specific, informed answer ("I've been following your migration to
     FoundationDB and the tradeoffs discussed in your 2023 engineering blog")
     is 10x stronger than "I like the culture."

  3. ALIGN BEHAVIORAL STORIES TO VALUES
     Amazon's 16 Leadership Principles (LPs) are used as the RUBRIC for scoring
     behavioral interviews at Amazon. Google has similar values.
     Research these values early and pre-map your STAR stories to them.

  4. UNDERSTAND THE PRODUCT AND TECH STACK
     In system design, saying "I'd store user data in DynamoDB" at Amazon is
     informed; at a startup it may be out of place. Know what they use.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. RESEARCH CHECKLIST (BY COMPANY)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Use this template for every company you interview with:

COMPANY: ______________________
ROLE: _________________________
DATE OF FIRST INTERVIEW: _______

A. COMPANY OVERVIEW
   [ ] What does the company do? (core product, business model)
   [ ] Revenue, growth stage (startup / growth / public)
   [ ] Recent news (acquisitions, layoffs, IPO, product launches)
   [ ] Key competitors and differentiators
   [ ] Culture reputation (Glassdoor, Blind, Levels.fyi, Reddit)

B. ENGINEERING CULTURE
   [ ] Engineering blog posts (last 6-12 months): key themes?
   [ ] Tech stack: What languages, databases, frameworks do they use?
   [ ] Scale: How many users? Requests per second? Data volumes?
   [ ] Open source contributions (GitHub org)
   [ ] Any famous technical papers or systems (Bigtable, Dynamo, MapReduce, etc.)

C. VALUES AND LEADERSHIP PRINCIPLES
   [ ] List the company's stated values (website, careers page, blog)
   [ ] Map each value to one of your STAR stories
   [ ] Are there values that show up repeatedly in Glassdoor reviews?
       (e.g., "move fast", "data-driven", "customer obsession")

D. INTERVIEW PROCESS
   [ ] What is the full interview loop? (phone screen → OA → on-site rounds)
   [ ] Number of rounds and format? (coding, SD, behavioral, case study)
   [ ] Typical timeline? (weeks from first contact to offer)
   [ ] LC difficulty level? (Easy/Medium/Hard — check company tag on LeetCode)
   [ ] Sources: Glassdoor, Blind, interviewing.io, LeetCode Discuss

E. TEAM/ROLE RESEARCH
   [ ] Job description: underline the key skills and responsibilities
   [ ] LinkedIn: Who's on the team? What did they work on before?
   [ ] Who's your hiring manager? Any tech talks, papers, LinkedIn posts?
   [ ] What's the team's core product/system you'd be working on?

F. PRODUCT EXPERIENCE
   [ ] Actually use the product if possible (sign up, explore)
   [ ] Note: UX pain points, features you'd prioritize, architecture questions
   [ ] Be ready to answer "What would you change about our product?"

3. COMPANY-SPECIFIC NOTES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

GOOGLE / ALPHABET
  Values: Googleyness (comfort with ambiguity, collaborative problem-solving)
  Interview format: 4-5 coding rounds + 1 Googleyness/leadership round
  Coding bar: Hard LC (Heap/DP/Graph); expect edge-case heavy questions
  SD: Very deep; focus on data model, consistency, scalability
  Behavioral: "Leadership and Googleyness" — tell me about conflict, ambiguity
  Key resources:
    - Google TechDev Guide: techdevguide.withgoogle.com
    - Google Engineering blog: developers.googleblog.com
  Mistake to avoid: Not asking good clarifying questions before coding

META / FACEBOOK
  Values: Move fast, be bold, focus on impact
  Interview format: 2 coding + 1 SD + 1 behavioral (product sense)
  Coding bar: Medium-Hard; often graph/DP; often 2 questions in 45 min
  SD: Emphasizes product + scale; expect "Design Instagram" type problems
  Behavioral: "Tell me about a project with ambiguous requirements"
  Key resources:
    - Meta Engineering blog: engineering.fb.com
    - RocksDB, TAO (Facebook's social graph DB), Cassandra contributions
  Mistake to avoid: Not having a product perspective in SD; ignoring user impact

AMAZON
  Values: 16 Leadership Principles — memorize them all
    (Customer Obsession, Ownership, Invent and Simplify, Are Right A Lot,
     Learn and Be Curious, Hire and Develop the Best, Insist on the Highest
     Standards, Think Big, Bias for Action, Frugality, Earn Trust,
     Dive Deep, Have Backbone, Deliver Results, Strive to be Earth's Best,
     Success and Scale)
  Interview format: 4-5 rounds; EVERY round includes LP-based behavioral questions
  Coding bar: Medium; LeetCode medium patterns; OA may include Hard
  SD: Emphasizes distributed systems (AWS); DynamoDB, S3, SQS frequently mentioned
  Behavioral: Prepare 2 STAR stories per LP; interviewers probe deeply
  Key resources:
    - Amazon working backwards (PR/FAQ approach)
    - AWS documentation and architecture whitepapers
  Mistake to avoid: Using "we" instead of "I" in behavioral stories

MICROSOFT
  Values: Growth mindset, collaboration, empowerment
  Interview format: 3-4 coding rounds + 1 "As Appropriate" hiring manager round
  Coding bar: Medium; sometimes design questions embedded in coding
  SD: More lenient than Google/Meta; strong emphasis on practical systems
  Behavioral: "Tell me about a time you helped a teammate learn"
  Key resources:
    - Microsoft Research blog
    - Azure architecture center
  Mistake to avoid: Not showing enthusiasm for learning and growth

APPLE
  Values: Craft, focus, privacy
  Interview format: Heavy on domain knowledge; expect deep dives into specific tech
  Coding bar: Medium-Hard; sometimes platform specific (iOS, macOS)
  SD: Smaller scale than FAANG; more product-focused
  Behavioral: "Tell me about a product you're proud of"
  Mistake to avoid: Being vague; Apple values precision and depth

STRIPE
  Values: Users first, move fast, rigorous
  Interview format: Systems design + coding + a "tech chat" (deep technical Q&A)
  Coding bar: Medium; focus on API design, clean code
  SD: Strong emphasis on reliability, payment systems, consistency
  Behavioral: Values rigor and intellectual curiosity
  Key resources:
    - Stripe Engineering blog: stripe.com/blog/engineering
    - Sorbet (Ruby type checker), Veneur, Bonsai
  Mistake to avoid: Weak on consistency guarantees in distributed systems

AIRBNB
  Values: Champion the mission, be an entrepreneur, be a host
  Interview format: Coding + SD + "cross-functional" behavioral
  Coding bar: Medium-Hard with emphasis on clean code and correctness
  SD: Expects strong product thinking embedded in design
  Behavioral: Must show genuine connection to Airbnb's mission

NETFLIX
  Values: Context over control, freedom and responsibility, inclusion
  Interview format: Mostly system design (especially for senior roles) + behavioral
  Coding bar: Lower; emphasis on reasoning, real-world trade-offs
  SD: Very deep; focused on large-scale streaming, availability, resilience
  Key resources:
    - Netflix Tech Blog: netflixtechblog.com (essential reading)
    - Chaos Monkey, Eureka, Zuul, Hystrix (Netflix OSS)
  Mistake to avoid: Over-structuring; Netflix prefers free-flowing deep conversation

STARTUPS / SERIES B+
  Interview format: Usually take-home + cultural + technical coding
  Coding bar: Variable; often practical (build a real feature, review real code)
  SD: Emphasize pragmatism over perfection; mention cost and velocity
  Behavioral: Very values-driven; "what motivates you?" is key
  Research focus: Product roadmap, engineering team size, tech debt indicators

4. HOW TO RESEARCH EFFECTIVELY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

STEP 1: START WITH THE JOB DESCRIPTION (JD)
  - Underline every technology mentioned (Java, Kafka, MySQL, etc.)
  - Note every responsibility ("design large-scale distributed systems" = SD focus)
  - Note required vs. preferred qualifications → gaps to address in prep

STEP 2: ENGINEERING BLOG (1-2 hours)
  - Read 3-5 recent posts (last 12 months) from the engineering blog
  - Look for: technologies mentioned, scale numbers, architectural patterns
  - Note any systems or problems directly relevant to the role

STEP 3: GLASSDOOR/BLIND/LEVELS.FYI (30 min)
  - Glassdoor: Read recent interview reviews for YOUR specific role
    → Note the exact problems mentioned, the format, the difficulty
  - Blind: Thread discussions about the company, the team
  - Levels.fyi: Benchmark compensation; understand band structure

STEP 4: LEETCODE COMPANY TAGS (30 min)
  - Search the company name on LeetCode → filter by frequency (last 6 months)
  - Note the top 10-15 problems → solve the ones you haven't seen

STEP 5: LINKEDIN (15 min)
  - Find your hiring manager (usually recruiter mentions them)
  - Look at their background, projects, open-source work
  - Search for team members: what roles do they come from?

STEP 6: PRODUCT USAGE (1-2 hours)
  - Create an account; use the core product
  - Ask yourself: How does this feature work technically?
  - Note 1-2 things you'd improve (useful for "what would you change?" question)

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5. THE "WHY THIS COMPANY" ANSWER FRAMEWORK
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Structure: (1) What drew you → (2) What you've discovered → (3) How you fit

TEMPLATE:
  "I've been following [Company] because of [specific product/technology/mission].
  When I was preparing for this interview, I read [specific blog post or paper]
  about [specific technical decision], and the way you're solving [problem] really
  resonated with me because [personal connection].
  Beyond the tech, your culture of [specific value you found in research] aligns
  with how I work best — [brief example]. I'd love to contribute to [specific team
  goal or product area] given my background in [relevant experience]."

EXAMPLE (for Stripe):
  "I've been fascinated by payment infrastructure challenges, and Stripe's
  engineering blog post about their Sorbet type system showed me the level
  of rigor you apply even to developer tools — not just the product.
  Reliability in payments is unforgiving, and that matches how I think about
  engineering. I'd bring my experience designing distributed transaction systems
  at my current company, where I led a migration to exactly-once Kafka delivery
  for our financial event pipeline."

BAD ANSWER (never say this):
  "I really like Stripe's culture and I've heard great things about the team."
