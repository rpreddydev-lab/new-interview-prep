1. WHAT IS THE INTERVIEW STRATEGY MODULE?
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

This module is the strategic layer on top of all your technical preparation.
Knowing data structures and algorithms is necessary but NOT sufficient to land
a job at a top-tier company. Interviewers evaluate:

  (a) Technical correctness - Does your solution work?
  (b) Communication - Can you explain your reasoning clearly?
  (c) Problem-solving process - How do you approach unknowns?
  (d) Culture fit / collaboration - Would teammates enjoy working with you?
  (e) Self-awareness - Do you recognize trade-offs and limitations?

This module teaches you HOW to present yourself and your thinking, not just
what to code.

2. THE FULL INTERVIEW PIPELINE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Most large tech companies (FAANG/MAANG, big tech, well-funded startups) follow
a predictable pipeline:

  STAGE 1: Recruiter / HR Screen (15-30 min)
  ------------------------------------------
  - Resume review, role fit, salary expectations
  - Basic behavioral questions ("Tell me about yourself")
  - Goal: Get to the technical screens

  STAGE 2: Online Assessment / Coding Challenge (60-90 min, async)
  ----------------------------------------------------------------
  - HackerRank, LeetCode, Codility, or proprietary platforms
  - Usually 2-3 medium LeetCode-style problems
  - May include debugging, output-prediction, or MCQ sections
  - Goal: Pass a bar to invite you to live interviews

  STAGE 3: Phone Screen / Technical Screen (45-60 min)
  ----------------------------------------------------
  - 1 coding problem (Easy-Medium) on a shared editor (CoderPad, etc.)
  - May include light system design or behavioral questions
  - Goal: Confirm technical competency before costly on-site

  STAGE 4: Virtual On-Site / On-Site Loop (3-6 rounds, each 45-60 min)
  ---------------------------------------------------------------------
  Typical breakdown:
    - 2-3 Coding/Algorithm rounds
    - 1-2 System Design rounds (for mid/senior+ levels)
    - 1 Behavioral / Leadership Principles round
    - 1 Hiring Manager / Culture fit round (sometimes)

  STAGE 5: Debrief and Hiring Committee
  --------------------------------------
  - Interviewers submit feedback (hire / no hire / mixed)
  - Hiring Committee reviews packet (may never have spoken to you)
  - Offer or rejection communicated by recruiter

  STAGE 6: Offer Negotiation
  ---------------------------
  - Base, equity (RSU/options), signing bonus, benefits
  - Negotiation window: typically 1-2 weeks
  - Multiple offers can be leveraged here

3. TYPES OF INTERVIEWS YOU WILL FACE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  A. ALGORITHM / CODING INTERVIEW
     - Focus: Data structures, algorithms, time/space complexity
     - Format: Write working code on shared editor or whiteboard
     - What they measure: Problem decomposition, code quality, optimization
     - Key mistake: Jumping to code before understanding the problem

  B. SYSTEM DESIGN INTERVIEW
     - Focus: Designing scalable distributed systems
     - Format: Open-ended discussion, whiteboard/diagram tools
     - What they measure: Architecture breadth, trade-off analysis, communication
     - Typical problems: Design Twitter, URL shortener, WhatsApp, YouTube
     - Key mistake: Not scoping requirements before diving into architecture

  C. BEHAVIORAL / LEADERSHIP INTERVIEW
     - Focus: Past experience, how you handle conflict/failure/success
     - Framework: STAR (Situation, Task, Action, Result)
     - What they measure: Ownership, communication, growth mindset
     - Key mistake: Vague answers without concrete metrics or outcomes

  D. TAKE-HOME ASSIGNMENT
     - Focus: Building a small feature, REST API, or mini-app in 24-72 hours
     - What they measure: Code quality, design choices, testing, README quality
     - Key mistake: Submitting without tests or a clear README

  E. TECHNICAL DEEP-DIVE / ARCHITECTURE REVIEW
     - Focus: Your past projects in detail
     - Format: Walk through a system you designed or built
     - What they measure: Depth of knowledge, ability to defend decisions

  F. ONLINE ASSESSMENT (OA)
     - Focus: Pure algorithmic problem-solving under time pressure
     - Often the first gate; fully automated
     - Key mistake: Not pacing yourself per problem

4. COMPANY TIER EXPECTATIONS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  TIER 1 (FAANG/MAANG - Meta, Apple, Amazon, Netflix, Google, Microsoft):
  -----------------------------------------------------------------------
  - Coding: Hard LeetCode problems expected, optimal solutions required
  - System Design: Large-scale distributed systems with detailed trade-offs
  - Behavioral: Amazon LP, Meta culture pillars, Google Googleyness
  - Bar: Extremely high; requires months of focused preparation

  TIER 2 (Stripe, Uber, Lyft, Airbnb, LinkedIn, Twitter/X, Snap, Dropbox):
  -------------------------------------------------------------------------
  - Coding: Medium-Hard; cleaner code and testing mindset emphasized
  - System Design: Strong fundamentals expected
  - Behavioral: Collaborative culture, past impact emphasized
  - Bar: High; 6-12 weeks of focused prep for most candidates

  TIER 3 (Series B/C startups, mid-size tech):
  --------------------------------------------
  - Coding: Medium difficulty; pragmatism valued over perfection
  - System Design: Simpler systems but must show architecture awareness
  - Behavioral: Culture fit, ability to work independently
  - Bar: Moderate; 2-6 weeks of focused prep

  TIER 4 (Enterprise, consulting, non-tech companies with tech roles):
  -------------------------------------------------------------------
  - Coding: Often SQL, basic algorithms, or language-specific questions
  - System Design: High-level only
  - Bar: Lower; standard preparation often sufficient

6. COMMON REASONS CANDIDATES FAIL (AND HOW TO AVOID THEM)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  FAILURE MODE 1: Silence during coding
  - Problem: Candidate codes for 10 minutes without speaking
  - Fix: Narrate your thought process continuously ("I'm thinking about...
    a HashMap here because lookups are O(1)...")

  FAILURE MODE 2: Jumping to code without understanding
  - Problem: Misunderstands the problem, writes wrong solution
  - Fix: Spend 3-5 minutes clarifying, give an example, confirm your
    understanding before writing a single line

  FAILURE MODE 3: Not handling edge cases
  - Problem: Solution breaks on null, empty arrays, negative numbers
  - Fix: Always ask "What if input is empty? null? single element?"

  FAILURE MODE 4: Brute force and done
  - Problem: Submits O(n²) when O(n log n) is expected
  - Fix: Always say "I have a brute force solution, but let me think about
    how to optimize it" before finalizing

  FAILURE MODE 5: System design too vague
  - Problem: "I'd use microservices and put it in the cloud" - no detail
  - Fix: Pick specific technologies, justify choices, discuss trade-offs

  FAILURE MODE 6: Behavioral answers with no results
  - Problem: "I worked on the team and things went better"
  - Fix: Always end with quantified results: "...reduced latency by 40%,
    saving $50k/month in infrastructure costs"

  FAILURE MODE 7: Freezing under pressure
  - Problem: Brain goes blank on a hard problem
  - Fix: See 01_Coding_Interview_Strategy/06_Stuck_Recovery_Strategies.txt

  FAILURE MODE 8: Not researching the company
  - Problem: Can't answer "Why do you want to work here?"
  - Fix: See 03_Technical_Interview_Prep/02_Company_Research.txt

  FAILURE MODE 9: Accepting first offer
  - Problem: Leaves 10-30% total compensation on the table
  - Fix: See 04_Offer_and_Negotiation/ folder

  FAILURE MODE 10: Interview fatigue / poor scheduling
  - Problem: Scheduling 6 companies simultaneously causes burnout
  - Fix: Stagger your pipeline; save dream companies for when sharp

7. MENTAL MODEL: WHAT INTERVIEWERS ARE ACTUALLY LOOKING FOR
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

From the interviewer's perspective, they are asking ONE question:
  "Would I want to work with this person every day?"

Sub-questions they're answering:
  - Can they solve hard problems independently?
  - Will they communicate blockers proactively?
  - Do they write code I can read and maintain?
  - Will they make the team better or drag it down?
  - Do they handle feedback gracefully?
  - Are they honest about what they don't know?

YOUR JOB during the interview:
  - Make the interviewer's job EASY. Give clear signals.
  - Think out loud so they can see your reasoning.
  - Ask clarifying questions so they know you care about correctness.
  - Recover gracefully from hints; never get defensive.
  - Show enthusiasm for the problem - engagement is contagious.

8. PREPARATION TIMELINE RECOMMENDATIONS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  IF YOU HAVE 1 WEEK:
  -------------------
  - Focus: LeetCode Easy/Medium (10/day), behavioral stories (3-5 stories),
    1 system design concept per day
  - Priority: Coding interview strategy + communication
  - Skip: Advanced system design, detailed Java deep dives

  IF YOU HAVE 1 MONTH:
  --------------------
  - Week 1: Coding fundamentals + strategy (arrays, strings, trees, graphs)
  - Week 2: Dynamic programming, sliding window, binary search patterns
  - Week 3: System design fundamentals + 5-6 design problems
  - Week 4: Mock interviews, behavioral prep, company research, negotiation

  IF YOU HAVE 3 MONTHS:
  ---------------------
  - Month 1: Full LeetCode Blind 75 + NeetCode 150 + system design basics
  - Month 2: Hard problems, advanced system design, behavioral story bank
  - Month 3: Mock interviews (3-4/week), company-specific prep, mental games

9. KEY PRINCIPLES TO INTERNALIZE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  PRINCIPLE 1: Communication > Correctness (in live interviews)
    A perfect silent solution is worse than a slightly imperfect but well-
    communicated one. Talk through your thinking.

  PRINCIPLE 2: Process > Product
    Interviewers can teach algorithms; they can't teach you how to think.
    Show a repeatable problem-solving process.

  PRINCIPLE 3: Confidence != Arrogance
    Confident = "I believe this approach is correct because X"
    Arrogant = "This is obviously the right approach"
    Be confidently humble.

  PRINCIPLE 4: The Interview is a Two-Way Street
    You are also evaluating THEM. Ask good questions at the end.
    This signals seniority and self-respect.

  PRINCIPLE 5: Recovery is a Skill
    Everyone gets stuck. The best candidates recover gracefully.
    Getting stuck is not failure. Staying stuck silently is.

  PRINCIPLE 6: Preparation is Respect
    Showing up underprepared signals you don't value the interviewer's time.
    Preparation is a form of professionalism.
