EXPERIENCE 1 — AMAZON (SDE III) — OFFER RECEIVED — March 2026
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Background: 9 years Java/Spring Boot. Applied to AWS Infrastructure team.

PROCESS:
  - OA: 2 LeetCode medium (70 min). Got: Rotting Oranges (BFS),
    and a variant of Jump Game II. Passed.
  - Phone Screen: LC 146 LRU Cache implementation + LP "Tell me about
    a time you disagreed with a stakeholder." Passed.
  - Loop (5 rounds, 1 day virtual):
    Round 1 (Coding): LC 239 Sliding Window Maximum + follow-up
                      variant with k-constraint. Hard difficulty.
    Round 2 (System): Design Amazon's Inventory Reservation System.
                      Focus: race conditions, distributed locking,
                      idempotency, exactly-once reservation.
    Round 3 (Coding): Graph problem — number of connected components
                      with union-find. Then: "What if edges have weights?"
    Round 4 (LP/Bar Raiser): All 16 LPs covered in 50 min. Deep dive on
                      "Dive Deep" and "Deliver Results" stories.
    Round 5 (System): Design a notification system (email/SMS/push)
                      with 100M users. Push notification fanout at scale.

KEY OBSERVATIONS:
  → Every round started with 15 min of behavioral (LP) questions
  → Bar Raiser asked: "Tell me a time you had to cut scope to meet deadline.
    How did you decide WHAT to cut?"
  → System design: Interviewer explicitly said "I want to see failure modes"
  → They pushed hard on "Dive Deep" — expected me to know my own system's
    numbers cold (req/sec, latency, DB size)

TIPS:
  → Prepare EXACTLY 16 LP stories (one per Amazon principle, no overlap)
  → In system design, proactively say "let me think about what breaks here"
  → "Dive Deep" = be ready to go from high-level to byte-level if asked

EXPERIENCE 2 — GOOGLE (L5) — OFFER RECEIVED — January 2026
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Background: 8 years Java backend. Strong DSA. PhD in CS dropped out.

PROCESS:
  - Phone Screen: 45 min. LC 127 Word Ladder (BFS). Had to give
    O(n*m*26) time complexity analysis. Got an optimized bidirectional BFS
    follow-up. Passed.
  - On-site Loop (5 rounds virtual):
    Round 1 (Coding): LC 295 Find Median from Data Stream.
                      Then: "What if the stream has only integers 0-100?"
                      (Bucket sort optimization). Hard follow-up.
    Round 2 (Coding): LC 297 Serialize/Deserialize Binary Tree.
                      Very clean code expected. Asked about edge cases.
    Round 3 (System): Design Google Maps routing at global scale.
                      Focus: graph algorithms (Dijkstra vs A*), real-time
                      traffic updates, pre-computation vs on-demand.
    Round 4 (Googleyness): "Tell me about a time you collaborated with
                      a team that had very different opinions." Deep probe
                      on HOW I built consensus.
    Round 5 (Coding): Custom problem — implement a thread-safe bounded
                      blocking queue without using java.util.concurrent.

KEY OBSERVATIONS:
  → Code quality is paramount at Google. Clean, well-structured code.
    Interviewers commented on variable naming and code readability.
  → Googleyness round: They want genuine intellectual curiosity.
    Was asked "What's an interesting technical problem you've been
    thinking about lately? Not work-related."
  → Hiring Committee (HC) does the final review. The interviewer is
    just an assessor — HC makes the offer/no-offer decision.
  → Leveling: LC hard problems solved cleanly = L5/L6 signal

TIPS:
  → Practice writing code without IDE — clean variable names matter
  → For Googleyness: have a genuine technical curiosity story ready
  → System design: Google loves O(n) analysis. Know your algorithm
    complexity for every design choice

EXPERIENCE 3 — META (E5) — REJECTED — December 2025
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Background: 7 years Java. Strong backend. Weak on DSA hard problems.

PROCESS:
  - Initial Screen: 2 LC problems in 45 min.
    Got: LC 1 Two Sum (easy warmup), LC 215 Kth Largest in Array (heap).
    Solved both. Passed.
  - On-site Loop (4 rounds):
    Round 1 (Coding): LC 239 Sliding Window Max + LC 480 Sliding Window Median.
                      Could not solve Median optimally in time. FAILED this round.
    Round 2 (System): Design Instagram's feed generation.
                      Discussion of fan-out on write vs read.
                      Interviewer pushed: "At 1B users, your fan-out-on-write
                      has celebrities with 100M followers. How do you handle it?"
    Round 3 (Behavioral): Standard STAR. "Move fast" culture. Told 2 stories.
    Round 4 (Coding): LC 23 Merge K Sorted Lists. Solved.

REJECTION REASON (from recruiter):
  "Strong system design and behavioral, but coding round 1 did not
   meet the bar. Meta expects LC medium solved in 15 min."

KEY OBSERVATIONS:
  → Meta expects FAST coding. They value speed of correct implementation.
  → Sliding window problems came up TWICE across different loops.
  → System design bar was fair — not as hard as Google's
  → Behavioral was lightweight — 2-3 questions max
  → Interviewers ran EXACTLY to time — no overtime allowed

LESSONS LEARNED:
  → Speed on LeetCode medium is non-negotiable at Meta
  → Practice solving on a timer: 15 min per medium problem
  → Sliding window and heap problems appear frequently — master them

Background: 10 years Java. 4 years at a hedge fund. Spring/JPA/Kafka expert.

PROCESS:
  - HackerRank OA: Java-specific MCQ (collections, threading, JVM) +
    1 medium coding (implement thread-safe bounded blocking queue).
  - Tech Phone Screen (60 min): Deep Java internals. No coding.
    Q: "Explain ConcurrentHashMap segment locking vs CAS-based locking in Java 8+"
    Q: "What happens during a Full GC? How does ZGC avoid STW pauses?"
    Q: "Explain volatile and the happens-before guarantee."
  - On-site Panel (4 rounds same day):
    Round 1 (Java Deep): 40 questions in 50 min. Rapid fire.
      Topics: volatile, synchronized, ThreadLocal, WeakReference,
      ClassLoader, Metaspace, G1 GC regions, CompletableFuture thread model
    Round 2 (System): Design a real-time trade risk calculation engine.
      Process 1M positions, recalculate P&L on market tick, sub-10ms latency.
    Round 3 (Domain): Financial domain knowledge.
      "What is a FIX protocol?" "What is settlement risk?"
      "How would you handle currency conversion precision?"
    Round 4 (MD/Behavioral): With a Managing Director.
      Questions about career growth, handling pressure, leading without authority.

KEY OBSERVATIONS:
  → Goldman goes MUCH deeper on Java internals than FAANG
  → They care about financial domain — even if you don't know it,
    show you can learn it: "I haven't worked directly with FIX,
    but I understand binary protocol design and low-latency messaging"
  → BigDecimal expertise is tested directly: "How would you calculate
    interest accrual in Java without floating-point errors?"
  → The MD round is a culture fit — be composed and articulate

TIPS:
  → Master: volatile, synchronized, CAS, ThreadLocal, WeakReference,
    CompletableFuture execution model — these are ALL tested at Goldman
  → Financial domain: idempotency, ACID, settlement, reconciliation
  → Speed is less important than correctness and depth here

Background: 8 years Java/Spring Boot. Banking domain. AWS experience.

PROCESS:
  - HackerRank: Spring Boot multiple-choice + 2 easy LC problems.
  - Technical Phone Screen (45 min):
    Q: "How does Spring @Transactional work internally with proxies?"
    Q: "What is the Saga pattern? Choreography vs orchestration?"
    Q: "How do you implement idempotency in a REST payment API?"
  - Panel Interview (3 rounds):
    Round 1 (Spring/Microservices): Entire round on Spring internals.
      "Walk me through what happens when you call a @Transactional method."
      "Why does @Transactional self-invocation not work?"
      "How does Spring Cloud Gateway differ from Zuul? What are the internals?"
    Round 2 (System Design): Design a payment reconciliation service.
      Handle 10M transactions/day. Detect discrepancies. Retry failed items.
      Generate regulatory reports.
    Round 3 (HR/Cultural): Standard behavioral. Values alignment.

KEY OBSERVATIONS:
  → Spring Framework internals are tested to a deeper level than at FAANG
  → Financial domain knowledge expected: reconciliation, T+2 settlement,
    idempotency, audit logs, immutable transaction records
  → System design focused on correctness and compliance, not just scale
  → Coding was not LeetCode-hard — correctness and approach matter more

TIPS:
  → Know @Transactional internals: proxy-based, self-invocation pitfall,
    propagation levels (REQUIRED, REQUIRES_NEW, NESTED)
  → Know Spring Security filter chain completely
  → Focus on financial domain: "How do you ensure no double-payment?"
