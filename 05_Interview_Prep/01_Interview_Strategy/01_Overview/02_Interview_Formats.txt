INTERVIEW FORMATS - A COMPLETE BREAKDOWN
================================================================================
Last Updated: April 2026
Purpose: Understand every interview format you will encounter so you can
         prepare specifically for each one - no surprises.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. ONLINE ASSESSMENT (OA)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

WHAT IT IS:
  An asynchronous timed coding challenge sent via email, usually before any
  human interaction. Companies use it to filter large applicant pools cheaply.

COMMON PLATFORMS:
  - HackerRank (most common)
  - Codility
  - LeetCode for Business
  - Karat (human-delivered but structured)
  - CoderByte
  - Pymetrics (game-based cognitive assessment)
  - HireVue (video + coding combo)

TYPICAL STRUCTURE:
  - Duration: 60-90 minutes (sometimes 120 min for senior roles)
  - Problems: 2-3 algorithmic problems (Easy + Medium + Medium-Hard)
  - May include: MCQs on CS fundamentals, debugging problems, SQL queries
  - No communication with anyone; fully automated

WHAT IS MEASURED:
  - Can you solve standard algorithmic problems under time pressure?
  - Code correctness (test cases pass/fail)
  - Sometimes: time to completion, number of compilation attempts
  - Some platforms track keystrokes/tab switching (avoid plagiarism)

SCORING:
  - Most OAs are pass/fail based on test case percentage
  - Aim for 100% on easy problems, 50-75%+ on hard ones
  - Partial credit: Many platforms give partial scores per test case
  - Edge cases matter: A solution that fails edge cases can fail you

STRATEGY:
  Step 1: Read ALL problems before starting. Allocate time proportionally.
  Step 2: Start with the easiest problem to build momentum.
  Step 3: For each problem: read → example → brute force → optimize.
  Step 4: Test with provided examples, then edge cases (null, empty, large n).
  Step 5: Submit when confident; move to next problem.
  Step 6: With 10 min left, go back and check failed edge cases.

COMMON MISTAKES:
  - Spending 70% of time on the hardest problem, failing easy ones
  - Not reading all constraints (leading to TLE or WA)
  - Forgetting to handle edge cases in time pressure
  - Writing code without testing it mentally first

PREPARATION:
  - Practice on HackerRank directly (UI familiarity matters)
  - Do timed practice: 45 min for 2 problems
  - Review common OA problem types: arrays, strings, trees, DP, graphs

2. PHONE SCREEN / TECHNICAL SCREEN
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

WHAT IT IS:
  A 45-60 minute live interview (video call) with an engineer from the company.
  Usually a middle step between OA and full on-site loop.

FORMAT:
  - 5 min: Introductions and role discussion
  - 35-40 min: 1-2 coding problems on a shared editor
  - 5-10 min: Your questions for the interviewer

SHARED EDITORS USED:
  - CoderPad (most common; supports running code)
  - HackerRank for Teams
  - CodeSignal
  - Google Docs (rare, but possible for whiteboard-style)
  - Zoom screen share + local IDE

WHAT IS MEASURED:
  - Technical competency: Can you solve a medium LeetCode problem?
  - Communication: Do you explain your approach before coding?
  - Collaboration: Do you take hints gracefully?
  - Code quality: Is your code clean, readable, correct?

PROBLEM DIFFICULTY:
  - Junior (0-3 years): Easy-Medium
  - Mid-level (3-6 years): Medium
  - Senior (6+ years): Medium-Hard or simpler design question added

STRATEGY:
  Step 1: Introduce yourself professionally (30-60 seconds, practiced).
  Step 2: When problem is given, read it aloud to confirm understanding.
  Step 3: Ask 2-3 clarifying questions (constraints, edge cases, examples).
  Step 4: State your approach in English before writing code.
  Step 5: Write clean, commented code while narrating.
  Step 6: Test with examples, then edge cases.
  Step 7: Analyze time and space complexity.
  Step 8: Discuss possible optimizations.

TIPS:
  - Use a real IDE setup with your preferred language
  - Have a glass of water nearby
  - Mute notifications on your computer
  - Use headphones for clear audio
  - Camera on (in almost all cases - shows engagement)

3. VIRTUAL ON-SITE LOOP
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

WHAT IT IS:
  A series of 4-6 back-to-back interviews (with breaks) conducted over video.
  This is the most intensive part of the process. Usually takes 4-6 hours total.

TYPICAL SCHEDULE (example):
  9:00 AM - Recruiter briefing (15 min)
  9:15 AM - Coding Round 1 (45 min)
  10:15 AM - Break (15 min)
  10:30 AM - Coding Round 2 (45 min)
  11:30 AM - Lunch break (30-60 min)
  12:30 PM - System Design Round (60 min)
  1:45 PM - Break (15 min)
  2:00 PM - Behavioral / Leadership Round (45 min)
  3:00 PM - Hiring Manager Round (30 min)
  3:30 PM - Close / Q&A with Recruiter

ROUNDS IN DETAIL:

  CODING ROUNDS (x2-3):
  ----------------------
  - Same as technical screen but higher difficulty
  - Problems: Medium-Hard (senior: potentially 2 Hard problems)
  - Expect follow-ups: "Can you do better?" / "What if input was..."
  - One interviewer per round (rotates)
  - Each interviewer submits independent feedback

  SYSTEM DESIGN ROUND (x1-2, mid/senior+):
  -----------------------------------------
  - Open-ended: "Design a URL shortener", "Design Instagram's feed"
  - 60 minutes: requirements → high-level design → deep dive → trade-offs
  - Whiteboard tool: Excalidraw, Miro, or built-in whiteboard
  - Not about one right answer; about how you think and communicate

  BEHAVIORAL ROUND (x1):
  ----------------------
  - STAR format questions (Situation, Task, Action, Result)
  - Typical questions:
    * "Tell me about a time you disagreed with your manager"
    * "Describe a project you're most proud of"
    * "Tell me about a failure and what you learned"
    * "How do you handle competing priorities?"
  - Amazon: 16 Leadership Principles specifically assessed
  - Meta: "Move fast", "Be bold", "Be direct", "Build social value"

  HIRING MANAGER ROUND (x1, sometimes):
  --------------------------------------
  - Mix of behavioral + light technical + vision questions
  - They want to know: Can I see this person on my team?
  - Good place to discuss your career goals and team interests

ENERGY MANAGEMENT:
  - Treat each round as independent; don't let a bad round cascade
  - Stay hydrated; have snacks for breaks
  - Don't check your performance after each round
  - If you go blank in one round, it doesn't mean you failed the loop
  - The hiring committee aggregates all feedback; one bad round can be forgiven

4. IN-PERSON / ON-SITE (RETURNING FORMAT)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

WHAT IT IS:
  Many companies are returning to in-person on-sites, especially for senior+
  roles. Same structure as virtual but at the company's office.

ADDITIONAL CONSIDERATIONS:
  - Dress: Business casual (unless the company culture is explicitly casual)
  - Arrive 15 minutes early; account for traffic/parking
  - Whiteboard coding: Practice writing code by hand (no autocomplete)
  - Bring multiple pens (black + different color for diagrams)
  - You may have lunch with the team (behavioral evaluation continues)
  - Reception is watching - be friendly to EVERYONE

WHITEBOARD TIPS:
  - Write large enough to be read from 3 feet away
  - Leave margin for variable tracking and examples
  - Number your steps
  - Don't erase unless you must; cross out instead
  - Use top-left to bottom-right flow

5. TAKE-HOME ASSIGNMENT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

WHAT IT IS:
  A project given to you with 24-72 hours to complete. Usually involves
  building a small application, REST API, CLI tool, or feature.

COMMON FORMATS:
  - "Build a REST API with CRUD for a given domain"
  - "Implement this algorithm and write tests"
  - "Review this code and improve it"
  - "Extend this existing codebase with a new feature"

TIME GUIDELINES:
  - Expected time: 4-8 hours (read the README carefully)
  - If it says 4 hours, submit in ~4 hours; more is not always better
  - Spend the first 30 min planning before writing any code

EVALUATION CRITERIA:
  - Does it compile and run?
  - Does it satisfy the stated requirements?
  - Is the code clean, modular, and readable?
  - Are there tests? (Unit + Integration)
  - Is there a README explaining how to run it?
  - Are there meaningful git commits (commit history tells a story)?
  - Error handling and edge cases considered?
  - No hardcoded secrets/credentials

STRATEGY:
  Step 1: Read all instructions twice before starting.
  Step 2: Plan the design (entities, APIs, data flow).
  Step 3: Set up project structure (standard layout for the language/framework).
  Step 4: Implement incrementally with clean commits.
  Step 5: Write tests as you go or right after each feature.
  Step 6: Write a clear README.md (setup, run, test, design decisions).
  Step 7: Review your code as if you're the interviewer. Refactor if needed.
  Step 8: Submit with a brief email summarizing what you built.

6. BEHAVIORAL / LEADERSHIP PRINCIPLES INTERVIEW
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

WHAT IT IS:
  A structured interview focused entirely on past behavior. Most companies
  believe "past behavior is the best predictor of future behavior."

FRAMEWORK: STAR
  S - Situation: Set the scene (company, team, timeframe)
  T - Task: What was your specific responsibility?
  A - Action: What did YOU specifically do? (not "we")
  R - Result: What was the measurable outcome?

COMMON QUESTION CATEGORIES:
  1. Leadership / Influence without authority
  2. Conflict resolution (with teammates, managers, stakeholders)
  3. Failure / Mistake and lessons learned
  4. Ambiguity / Working without clear direction
  5. Technical decision-making (design choices, trade-offs)
  6. Mentorship / Growing others
  7. Cross-functional collaboration
  8. Going above and beyond / Ownership

AMAZON LEADERSHIP PRINCIPLES (16 LPs - asked explicitly):
  - Customer Obsession
  - Ownership
  - Invent and Simplify
  - Are Right, A Lot
  - Learn and Be Curious
  - Hire and Develop the Best
  - Insist on the Highest Standards
  - Think Big
  - Bias for Action
  - Frugality
  - Earn Trust
  - Dive Deep
  - Have Backbone; Disagree and Commit
  - Deliver Results
  - Strive to be Earth's Best Employer
  - Success and Scale Bring Broad Responsibility

PREPARATION:
  - Prepare 8-10 strong STAR stories that can be reused across LPs
  - Each story should have quantifiable results
  - Practice out loud (record yourself)
  - Keep stories to 2-3 minutes each

7. KARAT INTERVIEWS (OUTSOURCED TECHNICAL SCREEN)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

WHAT IT IS:
  Some companies (DoorDash, Robinhood, etc.) outsource initial screens to
  Karat, a company of professional interviewers.

CHARACTERISTICS:
  - Very standardized; interviewers are professional (not from hiring company)
  - Questions tend to be consistent, structured, and well-paced
  - Feedback is forwarded to the hiring company
  - Usually 1-2 medium LeetCode problems
  - The interviewer will be helpful but professional

TIPS:
  - Same approach as a phone screen; treat it identically
  - The Karat interviewer is evaluating for the company's bar
  - Be extra communicative since the interviewer doesn't know your background

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
8. CASE STUDY / DESIGN PRESENTATION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

WHAT IT IS:
  More common at senior/staff/principal levels. You are asked to prepare a
  design or architecture presentation (30-60 min) for a panel.

FORMAT:
  - Given a problem (sometimes 24-48h in advance)
  - Present your solution as slides or diagrams
  - Q&A session with a panel of senior engineers/architects

TIPS:
  - Cover requirements → assumptions → constraints → design → trade-offs
  - Anticipate "why not X instead of Y?" questions
  - Show awareness of failure modes and scaling challenges
  - Be prepared to defend your choices without being defensive

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
9. PAIR PROGRAMMING INTERVIEW
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

WHAT IT IS:
  You and the interviewer work together on a real-world task in a shared IDE.
  More common at certain companies (e.g., Thoughtworks, some startups).

CHARACTERISTICS:
  - Less about optimal algorithm; more about collaboration style
  - They may write some code, ask you to continue, or debug together
  - Communication is everything here

TIPS:
  - Narrate what you're doing at all times
  - Ask "Does this approach make sense to you?" frequently
  - Don't take over; treat it as true pair programming
  - Show knowledge of shortcuts, debugging techniques, and IDE features
