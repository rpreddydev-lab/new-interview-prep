================================================================================
MOCK INTERVIEWS: HOW TO RUN THEM AND WHAT TO GET OUT OF THEM
================================================================================
Purpose: Mock interviews are the highest-leverage activity in interview prep.
         This guide covers HOW to run them effectively, what platforms to use,
         how to evaluate performance, and how to iterate.
================================================================================

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. WHY MOCK INTERVIEWS BEAT SOLO LEETCODE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The REAL interview is not about whether you know an algorithm.
It's about whether you can:
  - Perform under social pressure
  - Think out loud coherently
  - Recover from being stuck
  - Manage time across a full interview
  - Read the interviewer's hints and body language

None of these are tested by solo LeetCode practice.

A mock interview simulates ALL of these. The feedback gap between
"I solved this problem last night" and "I solved it with a stranger watching"
is enormous and must be closed before the real thing.

RULE: Do at least 5 mock interviews before your first target company interview.
      Do 10+ before your dream company interview.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. PLATFORMS FOR MOCK INTERVIEWS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

FREE OPTIONS:

  A. Pramp (https://pramp.com)
     - Peer-to-peer: you interview AND you are interviewed in each session
     - 45-60 minute sessions with real-time video + shared code editor
     - Problems are pre-assigned (focus: Data Structures & Algorithms,
       Frontend, Data Science, Behavioral)
     - BEST FOR: Getting volume of mocks at no cost
     - CAVEAT: Peer interviewers vary in quality; calibrate accordingly
     - HOW TO GET MOST OUT OF IT: Also play the interviewer role seriously —
       that's where you learn the most

  B. LeetCode Mock Interview Mode
     - Simulate timed interview within LeetCode
     - BEST FOR: Timing practice with real problems

  C. Peer Mocks with Friends/Colleagues
     - Find 1-2 peers also interviewing and swap mocks weekly
     - BEST FOR: Trusted, calibrated feedback from people who know your level

PAID OPTIONS:

  D. interviewing.io (https://interviewing.io)
     - Anonymous mock with real engineers from FAANG
     - $50-$200/session depending on tier
     - Feedback is extremely high-quality; detailed scorecards
     - BEST FOR: Calibration mock 2 weeks before target interview
     - STRATEGY: Use free Pramp mocks for volume; use interviewing.io
       for 1-2 high-quality calibration sessions

  E. Exponent (https://tryexponent.com)
     - Focus on system design and behavioral mocks
     - Community peer mocks are free; expert mocks are paid
     - BEST FOR: System design practice

  F. AlgoExpert (https://algoexpert.io)
     - Platform includes mock interview mode
     - BEST FOR: Structured problem progression + mock in same place

COMPANY-SPECIFIC:
  - Some companies (Google, Meta) offer mock interviews to candidates
    before on-sites — ASK YOUR RECRUITER if this is available
  - These are not evaluative; they give real signal for what to expect

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3. HOW TO RUN AN EFFECTIVE PEER MOCK
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

SETUP (5 minutes before):
  - Use Google Meet / Zoom with shared screen
  - Interviewer: Open a shared Google Doc or CoderPad (free)
  - Pick a problem the candidate has NOT seen before
    (Use LeetCode problems the candidate hasn't solved)
  - Set a timer: 45 minutes for coding, 50 minutes for system design

DURING (candidate guidelines):
  - Treat it EXACTLY like a real interview
  - Say "Hello, nice to meet you" — do the social warm-up
  - Don't ask to look up syntax — treat it as a real environment
  - If you know the exact answer, still walk through the thought process
  - If you're stuck, USE the recovery strategies (don't just ask for the answer)

DURING (interviewer guidelines):
  - Don't interrupt unless the candidate is truly blocked (>3 min silence)
  - Give the same hints a real interviewer would: "What if the input is empty?"
  - Do NOT give the answer directly; ask guiding questions
  - Note: clarifying questions asked, approach quality, time checkpoints,
    code quality, communication, edge cases missed

AFTER (debrief — 10-15 min):
  - CANDIDATE speaks first: "Here's what I think I did well / could improve"
  - INTERVIEWER gives structured feedback:
    1. What you did well (be specific: "You correctly identified the edge case...")
    2. What could be improved (be specific: "You didn't ask about data types...")
    3. Would I pass this interview? (Yes / No / Maybe — and WHY)
  - Write down the feedback IMMEDIATELY; don't rely on memory

4. SELF-EVALUATION SCORECARD (USE AFTER EVERY MOCK)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Rate each on a scale of 1-5 after every mock interview:

COMMUNICATION:
  [ ] Did I think out loud throughout? (1=silent, 5=narrated clearly)
  [ ] Did I ask good clarifying questions at the start? (1=jumped in, 5=thorough)
  [ ] Did I explain my approach before coding? (1=no, 5=fully articulated)
  [ ] Did I explain my thought process while coding? (1=silent, 5=running commentary)

PROBLEM SOLVING:
  [ ] Did I identify the right approach? (1=wrong approach, 5=optimal)
  [ ] Did I identify a brute force first? (1=no, 5=yes, then optimized)
  [ ] Did I recognize the pattern? (1=no, 5=immediate pattern recognition)

CODING:
  [ ] Was my code clean and readable? (1=messy, 5=professional)
  [ ] Did I write correct code without bugs? (1=multiple bugs, 5=clean first try)
  [ ] Did I handle edge cases in the code? (1=none, 5=all caught)
  [ ] Did I use appropriate variable names? (1=single letters, 5=descriptive)

TIME MANAGEMENT:
  [ ] Did I finish within the time limit? (1=ran out, 5=finished with 5+ min to spare)
  [ ] Did I spend too long on clarifications? (1=yes, 5=right balance)
  [ ] Did I dry run/test the code? (1=no, 5=thorough test with examples)

RECOVERY:
  [ ] When stuck, did I recover productively? (1=froze, 5=applied techniques)
  [ ] Did I maintain composure under pressure? (1=anxious/quiet, 5=calm/vocal)

TOTAL SCORE: ___/100

Track this score over time. A score below 70 means significant work needed.
A score of 80+ consistently means you're interview-ready.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5. THE "10 MISTAKES TO WATCH FOR" IN MOCKS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

1. JUMPING TO CODE WITHOUT CLARIFYING
   Signal: Interviewer says "what if the array is empty?" mid-code
   Fix: Always spend first 3-5 min on clarifying questions

2. SILENT CODING
   Signal: Interviewer says "can you walk me through what you're doing?"
   Fix: Narrate every decision while typing

3. OVER-CLARIFYING
   Signal: >5 min on questions before writing a single line
   Fix: Ask 2-3 key questions then start with your assumption stated aloud

4. GETTING LOCKED ON ONE APPROACH
   Signal: Still trying the same broken approach after 15 min
   Fix: At 10 min with no progress, explicitly say "let me step back"

5. NOT TESTING WITH EXAMPLES
   Signal: You say "I think it's correct" but didn't run through an example
   Fix: Always trace through at least 1 non-trivial example after coding

6. MISSING EDGE CASES
   Signal: Interviewer asks "what happens if k > n?"
   Fix: After coding, explicitly state "let me check edge cases" and go through list

7. PANICKING AFTER A HINT
   Signal: Interviewer gives a hint, you still seem flustered
   Fix: When you get a hint, say "thank you, let me think about that..." — pause 30 sec

8. WRONG COMPLEXITY ANALYSIS
   Signal: You say O(n) when it's clearly O(n²)
   Fix: Practice Big-O analysis on every problem you solve

9. MESSY CODE
   Signal: Interviewer has trouble reading your logic
   Fix: Use descriptive names; indent properly; separate concerns into helper methods

10. FORGETTING TO CLOSE WELL
    Signal: You say "I'm done" and sit quietly after finishing
    Fix: After finishing, invite feedback: "Does this approach look good to you?
         Is there anything you'd want me to revisit?"

6. TRACKING YOUR PROGRESS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

MOCK INTERVIEW LOG TEMPLATE:
  Date:          ___________
  Platform:      ___________
  Problem Name:  ___________
  Difficulty:    Easy / Medium / Hard
  Time Taken:    ___________
  Completed?     Yes / No / Partial
  Approach Used: ___________
  Bugs Found:    ___________
  Interviewer Feedback: ___________
  Score (from scorecard): ___/100
  Top 1 Thing to Improve: ___________

Keep a running spreadsheet of these. Review once/week.

LOOK FOR:
  - Is your average score trending up over time?
  - Are the SAME mistakes appearing repeatedly?
    → That's your highest-priority improvement target
  - Are you consistently running out of time?
    → Focus on coding speed and time management
  - Are you consistently unable to find the approach?
    → More pattern recognition practice needed

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
7. TYPES OF MOCK INTERVIEWS TO PRACTICE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

A. CODING MOCK (45 MIN)
   Structure:
     - 2 problems: 1 Medium (20 min) + 1 Medium/Hard (25 min)
     OR
     - 1 Hard problem (45 min for FAANG)
   What to measure: time to start, approach quality, bug count, edge cases

B. SYSTEM DESIGN MOCK (45-60 MIN)
   Structure:
     - 5 min: Requirements gathering
     - 10 min: High-level design
     - 20 min: Deep dive (pick 2 components)
     - 10 min: Trade-offs and Q&A
   Problems to use: Twitter, YouTube, Google Drive, Uber, WhatsApp

C. BEHAVIORAL MOCK (30 MIN)
   Structure:
     - 5-7 STAR questions from the interviewer
     - Candidate answers; interviewer probes with follow-ups
   Use: Amazon LP questions, Meta values questions, general leadership questions

D. FULL LOOP SIMULATION (3-4 HOURS)
   Do this once or twice before an on-site:
     - 45 min coding mock
     - 45 min system design mock
     - 30 min behavioral mock
     - 15 min debrief
   Simulate the fatigue of a real interview day.
