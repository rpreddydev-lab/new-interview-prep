================================================================================
MENTORING AND GROWING OTHERS — INTERVIEW PREPARATION
================================================================================
Purpose: At 7+ years, interviewers explicitly look for evidence that you
         raise the bar for those around you — not just for yourself.
         This is a required signal for Senior, Staff, and Principal levels.
================================================================================

WHY MENTORING IS ASSESSED AT SENIOR LEVEL
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Companies hire senior engineers not just for their output, but for their
multiplier effect on others. A senior engineer who codes well but hoards
knowledge is less valuable than one who makes their whole team better.

Amazon LP: "Hire and Develop the Best"
Google:    "Supports the growth of teammates" (Googleyness signal)
Meta:      "Execution — helps others around them grow"

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
TYPES OF MENTORING INTERVIEWERS LOOK FOR
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

  1. TECHNICAL MENTORING
     Helping junior/mid engineers grow their technical depth
     - Code reviews that teach, not just approve/reject
     - Pair programming sessions on complex problems
     - Explaining "why" behind architectural decisions, not just "what"

  2. CAREER MENTORING
     Helping engineers navigate their growth path
     - 1:1 check-ins, goal setting, feedback
     - Sponsoring junior engineers for visible opportunities
     - Helping them prepare for promotion / interviews

  3. KNOWLEDGE SCALING
     Making yourself less of a bottleneck
     - Writing runbooks, architecture docs, onboarding guides
     - Running tech talks, workshops, internal training
     - Creating reusable libraries/frameworks others can use

  4. HIRING CONTRIBUTION
     Raising the hiring bar for the team
     - Designing interview questions and rubrics
     - Conducting interviews and calibrating assessments
     - Writing accurate, actionable feedback on candidates

COMMON INTERVIEW QUESTIONS — MENTORING
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Q: Tell me about a time you mentored a junior engineer.
   → What was their growth area? How did you approach it?
   → What changed as a result? Did they reach a milestone / get promoted?

Q: How do you conduct code reviews?
   → What do you look for? How do you give feedback constructively?
   → How do you balance quality vs velocity?

Q: Tell me about a time you helped a struggling teammate.
   → Was it technical or interpersonal? What was the outcome?

Q: How do you ensure knowledge doesn't get siloed on your team?
   → What systems/processes have you put in place?

Q: Describe a time you helped an engineer get promoted or achieve a goal.
   → What specifically did you do to support them?

Q: How do you onboard new engineers to a complex system?
   → Documentation strategy, pairing schedule, ramp-up milestones

SAMPLE STRONG ANSWER — MENTORING STORY
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Q: Tell me about a time you mentored a junior engineer.

SITUATION:
  "A junior engineer on my team, Alex, had strong coding skills but
   struggled with system design thinking. He would implement features
   correctly at the method level but didn't consider failure modes,
   load, or downstream impact."

TASK:
  "As the senior engineer on the squad, I took it upon myself to
   help him develop this skill — it wasn't assigned to me, but I
   knew it was holding him back from promotion."

ACTION:
  "I started by including him in all my system design whiteboard sessions
   and asking him questions like 'what happens if this service goes down?'
   or 'how would this behave under 10x load?' I gave him a reading list
   starting with DDIA and Chapter 4 of Designing Distributed Systems.
   Every Friday we had a 30-minute 'design review' where he'd walk me
   through a design he'd done that week, and I'd ask probing questions."

RESULT:
  "Within 4 months, Alex was leading design reviews independently and
   was promoted to mid-level 6 months earlier than expected.
   He's now mentoring a new junior engineer himself."

CODE REVIEW PHILOSOPHY — WHAT TO SAY IN INTERVIEWS
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

"I approach code reviews with three goals:

  1. CORRECTNESS: Does this code behave correctly for all inputs,
     including edge cases and failure scenarios?

  2. MAINTAINABILITY: Will the next engineer (or me in 6 months)
     understand this code without needing the author to explain it?

  3. GROWTH: I always try to explain the 'why' behind my suggestions,
     not just point out problems. A comment like 'this could cause a
     deadlock if two threads acquire these locks in different order —
     here's why and how to fix it' is more valuable than 'change this.'

  I distinguish between blocking issues (correctness, security, data loss risk)
  and non-blocking suggestions (style, optimization ideas).
  I don't block PRs on personal preference — only on objective issues."

ONBOARDING STRATEGY — WHAT SENIOR ENGINEERS BUILD
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Interviewers love to hear you've built systems that scale knowledge:

  ✅ "I wrote a 30-60-90 day onboarding guide for new team members"
  ✅ "I created architecture decision records (ADRs) for every major
      tech decision so new engineers understand why things are the way they are"
  ✅ "I ran a monthly 'Intro to our Kafka pipeline' workshop for new joiners"
  ✅ "I automated the local dev setup from 2 days of manual config to a
      single docker-compose command"
