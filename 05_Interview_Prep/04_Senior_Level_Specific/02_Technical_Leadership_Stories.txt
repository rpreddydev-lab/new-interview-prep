WHAT IS TECHNICAL LEADERSHIP?
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Technical leadership is NOT just managing people.
It means driving the technical direction of a project or team —
making high-impact decisions, setting standards, unblocking others,
and taking accountability for outcomes.

Core dimensions:
  1. Technical Vision    — setting direction and making architectural choices
  2. Technical Influence — guiding team decisions without formal authority
  3. Quality Culture     — establishing code standards, review practices, testing
  4. Risk Management     — anticipating tech debt, failures, and scaling limits
  5. Knowledge Transfer  — ensuring the team grows alongside the codebase
  6. Cross-team Impact   — driving decisions beyond your immediate team

COMMON INTERVIEW QUESTIONS — TECHNICAL LEADERSHIP
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Q: Tell me about a time you led a significant technical decision.
   → What was the decision? Who did you consult? How did you weigh trade-offs?
   → What was the impact? Would you make the same decision again?

Q: Describe a time you had to convince your team to adopt a new technology
   or change an existing architecture.
   → How did you build consensus? How did you handle resistance?

Q: Tell me about a time you identified a critical technical risk early.
   → How did you surface it? What did you do? What was avoided?

Q: Tell me about a time you had to make a technical decision under time pressure
   with incomplete information.
   → How did you decide? What trade-offs did you accept?

Q: Describe a technical decision you regret. What would you do differently?
   → Demonstrates self-awareness and continuous learning.

Q: How do you ensure the quality bar stays high on your team?
   → Talk about code reviews, testing standards, CI/CD gates, documentation.

Q: Give an example of when you influenced engineering decisions across teams.
   → Cross-functional impact is a key signal for Staff/Principal level.

BUILDING YOUR TECHNICAL LEADERSHIP STORIES (STAR FORMAT)
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

STORY TEMPLATE:
  Situation:  What was the technical context, team size, and challenge?
  Task:       What was YOUR specific responsibility or leadership role?
  Action:     Exactly what did YOU do? (include alternatives considered,
              how you influenced others, how you mitigated risk)
  Result:     Measurable outcome: latency ↓, throughput ↑, incidents ↓,
              dev velocity ↑, cost $, team growth

MUST-HAVE STORIES (prepare at least 1 per category):

  1. Architecture Decision
     Example: "I led the migration from a monolith to microservices.
               We split the order service first based on bounded context analysis.
               I defined the API contracts, migration phases, and rollback plan.
               Result: 40% reduction in deployment time, independent scaling."

  2. Driving Adoption of a New Technology
     Example: "I introduced Kafka for async event processing.
               Ran an internal tech talk, wrote a proof-of-concept, and
               presented latency benchmarks to the team.
               Result: reduced API response time from 800ms to 120ms."

  3. Quality / Tech Debt Initiative
     Example: "Identified 3-year-old untested payment module as high risk.
               Advocated for a 2-sprint refactor, wrote test coverage plan,
               got stakeholder buy-in by framing it as risk reduction.
               Result: went from 12% to 87% test coverage; eliminated 2 P1 bugs."

  4. Cross-Team Technical Influence
     Example: "Two teams were implementing duplicate caching solutions.
               I proposed a shared Redis-based caching layer.
               Facilitated design sessions, drafted RFC, got alignment.
               Result: eliminated 3 months of duplicated work."

  5. Technical Decision Under Pressure
     Example: "During a production incident at 2am, the primary DB was failing.
               I made the call to failover to the read replica in write mode.
               Accepted the risk of slight data inconsistency for availability.
               Result: recovered in 8 minutes; post-mortem led to better runbooks."

INFLUENCE WITHOUT AUTHORITY
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

As a senior IC (Individual Contributor), you often don't have direct reports,
but you're expected to drive decisions. Key techniques:

  DATA-DRIVEN ARGUMENTS
    → Use benchmarks, incident data, cost analysis, adoption metrics
    → "I ran a benchmark showing Caffeine cache reduced DB load by 60%"

  WRITE RFCs / DESIGN DOCS
    → A written proposal forces clarity and invites structured feedback
    → Shows you can think rigorously and communicate asynchronously

  BUILD PROTOTYPES
    → A working proof-of-concept beats 10 slides every time
    → "I built a weekend prototype showing the new approach was viable"

  FIND ALLIES FIRST
    → Align 1-2 senior engineers before a broader discussion
    → "I talked to Sarah and David individually first; they both agreed"

  FRAME IN BUSINESS TERMS
    → Link technical decision to reliability, speed to market, or cost
    → "This refactor reduces our mean time to recovery by an estimated 40%"

WHAT SIGNALS "STAFF+" LEVEL
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

  ✅ Impact spans multiple teams or the entire org
  ✅ Proactively identifies problems before they are assigned
  ✅ Designs systems that others build on for years
  ✅ Raises the technical bar of everyone around them
  ✅ Makes hard trade-off calls that stick under pressure
  ✅ Knows when NOT to build something (buy vs build, scope)
  ✅ Is the "go-to" person across the organization for a technical area
