WHAT CHANGES AT THE SENIOR LEVEL
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

At 7+ years, interviewers evaluate you differently from junior/mid candidates.

CODING:
  - Expected: Clean, idiomatic Java. No syntax mistakes.
  - Expected: Immediately identify optimal approach with complexity analysis.
  - Trap:     Brute force is no longer acceptable as a "starting point."
  - Standard: Medium problems in 15-20 min. Hard in 30-40 min.

SYSTEM DESIGN:
  - Expected: Lead the conversation, not just answer questions.
  - Expected: Proactively raise scale, failure, consistency trade-offs.
  - Expected: Talk about what you personally designed/built — not textbook.
  - Trap:     Generic answers without real-world grounding will fail.

BEHAVIORAL:
  - Expected: Stories about leading initiatives, not just participating.
  - Expected: Evidence of cross-team impact, not just team-level impact.
  - Expected: How you mentor, grow others, and build team culture.
  - Trap:     Saying "I" when you should say "we" — and vice versa.

TECHNICAL DEPTH:
  - Expected: Go 3-4 levels deep on any claim you make.
  - Expected: Know the "why" behind every tool/framework you use.
  - Expected: Have opinions and defend them with data/experience.
  - Trap:     "I used Spring Boot" without knowing what it does internally.

SENIOR INTERVIEW LOOP — TYPICAL STRUCTURE
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Most senior loops (5-6 rounds) include:
  1. Recruiter Screen         — experience, comp expectations, availability
  2. Technical Phone Screen   — 1 DSA coding + quick Java/design questions
  3. System Design Round      — high-level + component deep-dive (45-60 min)
  4. DSA Coding Round 1       — 2 problems (45 min)
  5. DSA Coding Round 2       — 2 problems + follow-up discussion
  6. Behavioral / Leadership  — STAR stories, leadership signals
  [7. Bar Raiser / Hiring Committee — at Amazon, Google, Meta]

KEY INSIGHT: At senior level, behavioral and technical depth carry equal weight.
             Failing behavioral alone can reject an otherwise strong candidate.

SENIOR-SPECIFIC EVALUATION DIMENSIONS
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

  1. TECHNICAL BREADTH + DEPTH
     Can you go deep on your claims and wide across the stack?

  2. ARCHITECTURAL THINKING
     Can you design systems from scratch with scale and reliability in mind?

  3. TECHNICAL LEADERSHIP
     Have you led technical direction, made high-impact decisions,
     mentored others, and driven engineering culture?

  4. CROSS-FUNCTIONAL COLLABORATION
     Can you work across teams, influence without authority, and
     communicate technical complexity to non-engineers?

  5. OWNERSHIP AND ACCOUNTABILITY
     Do you own outcomes, not just tasks? Do you handle failures
     proactively and learn from them?

  6. AMBIGUITY HANDLING
     Can you make sound decisions with incomplete information?
     Do you know when to gather more data vs. act decisively?

COMMON REASONS SENIOR CANDIDATES FAIL
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

  ❌ No leadership stories — all stories are individual contributor
  ❌ System design too vague — never reaches component-level detail
  ❌ Cannot go deep on their own tech stack ("I just used Spring")
  ❌ Weak behavioral answers — no structure, no measurable impact
  ❌ LeetCode problems solved slowly or with suboptimal solutions
  ❌ No trade-off discussions — only describe one approach
  ❌ Defensiveness when asked follow-up or challenging questions
  ❌ Resume/background doesn't match the answers given

  ✅ FIX: Prepare "depth stories" — pick 3-4 projects and know them
          inside out (architecture, trade-offs, your specific role,
          what went wrong, what you'd change, scale/impact numbers).
