================================================================================
YOUR "WALK ME THROUGH YOUR RESUME" — SENIOR JAVA DEVELOPER
================================================================================
Purpose: The opening question of almost every interview. At senior level,
         this is your first impression and sets the entire tone.
         A weak opener signals a weak candidate regardless of skills.
================================================================================

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
THE FORMULA FOR A STRONG 2-MINUTE PITCH
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Structure: [Experience summary] → [Key technical strengths] →
           [Highest-impact project] → [Why here/what's next]

TEMPLATE:

  "I'm a Java backend engineer with [X] years of experience building
   [type of systems: distributed/high-scale/fintech/microservices].

   My core strengths are [2-3 specific: e.g., Java/Spring Boot,
   distributed systems design, Kafka-based event-driven architecture].

   Most recently at [Company], I [led/designed/built] [specific system
   with scale numbers]. The work I'm most proud of is [1 sentence on
   highest-impact project with measurable outcome].

   I'm looking to join [this company / a team] where I can [specific goal:
   build at scale / work on complex distributed systems / join a team
   with strong engineering culture] because [genuine reason]."

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
WHAT STRONG LOOKS LIKE VS WEAK
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

❌ WEAK:
  "I have 8 years of experience in Java. I've worked at XYZ company
   where I worked on various projects using Spring Boot, Microservices,
   REST APIs, Kafka, Redis, and many other technologies. I'm looking
   for new opportunities."

  Problems: Generic, no specifics, no impact, no story arc, no hook.

✅ STRONG:
  "I'm a senior Java engineer with 8 years of experience specializing
   in high-throughput distributed systems. At my current company, I
   led the design and migration of our monolithic payment platform to a
   microservices architecture handling 25K transactions per second at
   99.99% uptime. I own the Kafka-based event pipeline that processes
   $500M in daily transaction volume.

   Before that, I built the real-time fraud detection service that
   reduced fraudulent transactions by 35% in the first quarter.

   I'm exploring opportunities because I want to work at scale on
   technically challenging infrastructure problems, and what [Company]
   is building in [specific domain] genuinely excites me."

WRITE YOUR OWN 2-MINUTE PITCH BELOW
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

DRAFT (fill this in with your real experience):

  "I'm a __________ engineer with _____ years of experience [in what domain].

   My core expertise is in ________________________ [2-3 specific things].

   Most recently at __________, I led/built/designed __________________
   which served ________ users / handled ________ req/sec /
   processed $________ / reduced latency by _______.

   The project I'm most proud of is ________________________________
   because _________________________________________________________.

   I'm exploring new opportunities because _________________________,
   and I'm particularly interested in [this company] because __________."

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
KEY RULES FOR YOUR PITCH
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

  ✅ Keep it to 90-120 seconds — not 30 sec (too short) or 5 min (too long)
  ✅ Always include at least ONE number (scale, impact, users, latency)
  ✅ End with a forward-looking statement (where you want to go)
  ✅ Tailor the pitch for each company — mention why THEM specifically
  ✅ Practice out loud — fluency matters as much as content
  ✅ Do NOT read from memory — know it well enough to say naturally

  ❌ Don't list every technology you've touched
  ❌ Don't mention early-career junior work unless it's highly relevant
  ❌ Don't badmouth previous employers
  ❌ Don't be vague about what you personally contributed vs the team

FOLLOW-UP QUESTIONS AFTER YOUR PITCH
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

After your pitch, the most common follow-ups are:

  → "Tell me more about [project X you mentioned]"
     → This is your entry point to a deep technical story.
     → Have the full STAR + architecture tour ready for your top 2-3 projects.

  → "What's the most complex technical challenge you've faced?"
     → Pick your strongest architecture/leadership story.

  → "Why are you leaving your current role?"
     → Keep it positive and forward-looking.
     → "I've grown a lot at [Company] and I'm ready for a bigger challenge
        in [specific area]. [Company you're interviewing at] is working on
        [specific problem] that I find genuinely exciting."

  → "Why this company?"
     → Be specific — mention a product, engineering blog post, open-source
        contribution, or technology they use that you've researched.
     → Vague answers like "great culture and good pay" signal no genuine interest.
