NETFLIX — INTERVIEW PREPARATION (Senior Software Engineer)
================================================================================
Culture Focus: Freedom & Responsibility, High Performance, Context not Control
Levels:  E4 (Senior Engineer), E5 (Senior+), E6 (Principal)
================================================================================

NETFLIX CULTURE — "FREEDOM & RESPONSIBILITY"
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Netflix operates differently from every other tech company.
Their famous "Culture Deck" (originally by Patty McCord + Reed Hastings)
defines their expectations:

  HIGH PERFORMANCE:
    → Netflix hires the very best and expects extraordinary output
    → The famous line: "We're a team, not a family."
    → Adequate performance earns a generous severance package
    → They do not tolerate "brilliant jerks" — excellence AND values

  FREEDOM & RESPONSIBILITY:
    → No approval processes for most decisions — engineers decide
    → "Act in Netflix's best interest" — you're trusted to do the right thing
    → No vacation policy (take what you need), limited bureaucracy
    → With freedom comes accountability — own your outcomes

  CONTEXT, NOT CONTROL:
    → Managers provide context (goals, constraints, strategy)
    → Engineers make their own decisions within that context
    → "Highly aligned, loosely coupled" teams

  CANDOR:
    → Direct, honest feedback — even uncomfortable truths
    → "I disagree with that approach because..." is encouraged
    → They call it "farming for dissent"

INTERVIEW PROCESS
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

  Round 1: Recruiter Screen (experience, compensation alignment)
  Round 2: Hiring Manager Screen (technical depth + culture fit)
  Round 3-5: Virtual Loop (5-7 rounds)
    - 2-3 Coding (complex, often multi-part)
    - 1-2 System Design (large-scale, Netflix-specific scenarios)
    - 1 Culture Fit (values, candor, decision-making style)
    - 1 Domain Deep Dive (Java/distributed systems depth)

  NOTE: Netflix interviews are widely considered among the hardest
        in the industry. System design is evaluated at an exceptionally
        high bar — they expect production-level thinking.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
BEHAVIORAL — CULTURE FIT QUESTIONS
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

  Q: Tell me about a time you had to make a critical decision with no
     management approval or guidance.
     → Tests Freedom & Responsibility — can you own decisions without oversight?

  Q: Give an example of candid feedback you gave to a peer or manager.
     → Direct communication is a core Netflix value
     → Show you can deliver hard truths constructively

  Q: Tell me about a time you disagreed with a decision but followed through anyway.
     → Disagree and commit — after voicing disagreement, you execute loyally

  Q: How do you prioritize when you have unlimited things to do?
     → No one will tell you what to do — show judgment and autonomy

  Q: Tell me about a failure you caused. What happened and what did you do?
     → Candor — they want real stories, not sanitized ones

  Q: Tell me about a time you handled an ambiguous situation with minimal guidance.
     → High autonomy culture — they want self-starters

  Q: How do you share context with your team so everyone can make good decisions?
     → "Context, not control" — they want engineers who write docs, share learnings

TECHNICAL — JAVA AND DISTRIBUTED SYSTEMS AT NETFLIX SCALE
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Netflix OSS (many are Java-based, worth knowing):
  → Eureka       — service discovery (Spring Cloud Eureka)
  → Hystrix       — circuit breaker (now superseded by Resilience4j)
  → Ribbon        — client-side load balancing
  → Zuul          — API Gateway (now Spring Cloud Gateway preferred)
  → Conductor    — microservice orchestration platform
  → Chaos Monkey — resilience testing (Simian Army)
  → Hollow        — data publishing infrastructure
  → Iceberg       — open table format for big data

TECHNICAL QUESTIONS COMMONLY ASKED:
  Q: How does Netflix handle service failures gracefully? Walk through
     the circuit breaker pattern and the states in detail.
  Q: Explain how you'd build a highly available recommendation engine
     serving 250M users in 190 countries.
  Q: How does Netflix ensure content loads in < 2 seconds globally?
     (CDN architecture, Open Connect Appliances)
  Q: How would you design a system to detect and handle "thundering herd"
     problems in a distributed cache at Netflix scale?
  Q: Explain chaos engineering. How would you implement controlled failures
     in a Java microservices environment?

CODING:
  → LeetCode Hard difficulty is expected
  → Often multi-part problems with follow-up optimizations
  → May involve custom data structure design
  → Expect deep complexity analysis and code cleanliness

SYSTEM DESIGN (at massive scale):
  → Design Netflix's video streaming pipeline
  → Design a global A/B testing system (Netflix uses heavily)
  → Design Netflix's recommendation system (content + user graph)
  → Design a real-time analytics pipeline for 250M streams simultaneously

TIPS FOR NETFLIX INTERVIEWS
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

  1. CANDOR is the most important culture signal. Give direct opinions.
     Say "I think X is better because Y" — not "it depends" hedging.

  2. OWNERSHIP and AUTONOMY. Every story should show you drove
     outcomes without being told exactly what to do.

  3. Prepare for questions about FAILURE with brutal honesty.
     Netflix respects engineers who own their mistakes clearly.

  4. Know Netflix tech: Chaos Monkey, Eureka, A/B testing at scale,
     CDN architecture. Read the Netflix Tech Blog before the interview.
     (netflixtechblog.com)

  5. System design must go DEEP. Expect the interviewer to push you
     into component-level detail (exactly how the Kafka consumer
     manages offsets in your design, etc.)

  6. Compensation is top-of-market. Come with a specific number,
     not a range. Netflix respects directness in negotiation too.
