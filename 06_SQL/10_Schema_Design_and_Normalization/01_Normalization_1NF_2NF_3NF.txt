================================================================
  NORMALIZATION: 1NF, 2NF, 3NF
================================================================

================================================================
FIRST NORMAL FORM (1NF)
================================================================

  Rules:
  1. Each column contains ATOMIC (indivisible) values
  2. No repeating groups or arrays in columns
  3. Each row is unique (has a primary key)

  VIOLATION: non-atomic
  student_id  name    courses
  ----------  ------  ----------------------------
  1           Alice   Math, Science, History        <-- multi-value

  FIX (1NF):
  student_id  name    course
  ----------  ------  -------
  1           Alice   Math
  1           Alice   Science
  1           Alice   History

  Or better: separate courses table with FK.

  VIOLATION: repeating groups
  order_id  item1   price1  item2   price2
  --------  ------  ------  ------  ------
  1         Widget  9.99    Gadget  19.99

  FIX: order_items table with (order_id, product, price) rows.

================================================================
SECOND NORMAL FORM (2NF)
================================================================

  Rules:
  1. Is in 1NF
  2. Every non-key attribute is FULLY FUNCTIONALLY DEPENDENT on
     the ENTIRE primary key (no partial dependencies)
  Applies only when PK is COMPOSITE.

  VIOLATION:
  Table: order_items(order_id, product_id, qty, product_name, product_price)
  PK = (order_id, product_id)

  product_name depends on product_id alone (not on full composite PK).
  product_price depends on product_id alone.
  qty depends on (order_id, product_id) -- this is fine.

  PARTIAL DEPENDENCIES: product_name, product_price on product_id only.

  FIX (2NF):
  order_items(order_id, product_id, qty)          -- qty fully depends on composite PK
  products(product_id, product_name, product_price) -- product info in own table

================================================================
THIRD NORMAL FORM (3NF)
================================================================

  Rules:
  1. Is in 2NF
  2. No TRANSITIVE DEPENDENCIES: no non-key attribute depends on another
     non-key attribute

  VIOLATION:
  Table: employees(emp_id, name, dept_id, dept_name, dept_location)
  emp_id --> dept_id (direct dependency on PK)
  dept_id --> dept_name, dept_location (transitive: through dept_id)

  emp_id --> dept_id --> dept_name   <-- transitive dependency

  ANOMALIES:
  Update dept_name: must update every employee row in that dept.

  FIX (3NF):
  employees(emp_id, name, dept_id)
  departments(dept_id, dept_name, dept_location)

================================================================
PRACTICAL EXAMPLE: BUILDING FROM UNNORMALIZED
================================================================

  Unnormalized (UNF):
  order_id  customer_name  customer_email   product   qty   category   cat_manager
  --------  -------------  ---------------  --------  ---   --------   -----------
  1         Alice          a@example.com    Widget    2     Electronics Tom
  1         Alice          a@example.com    Gadget    1     Electronics Tom
  2         Bob            b@example.com    Widget    1     Electronics Tom

  Step 1 - 1NF: Already atomic; add PK (order_id, product_id)

  Step 2 - 2NF: Separate out customer data (depends only on order_id, not product)
  orders(order_id, customer_name, customer_email)
  order_items(order_id, product, qty, category, cat_manager)

  Step 3 - 3NF: Separate transitive dependencies
  orders(order_id, customer_id)
  customers(customer_id, customer_name, customer_email)
  order_items(order_id, product_id, qty)
  products(product_id, product_name, category_id)
  categories(category_id, category_name, cat_manager)

================================================================
INTERVIEW Q&A
================================================================

  Q: What is 2NF and when does it apply?
  A: Second Normal Form applies to tables with composite primary keys.
     It requires that every non-key column depends on the ENTIRE key,
     not just part of it. Partial dependencies (column depends on only
     one column of a composite PK) must be moved to a separate table.

  Q: What is a transitive dependency?
  A: When a non-key column A depends on another non-key column B,
     which depends on the PK. A --> B --> PK.
     3NF eliminates this by moving A and B to a separate table keyed by B.

  Q: What anomalies does 3NF eliminate?
  A: Update anomaly (change one fact in multiple rows),
     insertion anomaly (can't record a fact without another fact),
     deletion anomaly (deleting one fact accidentally deletes another).
