QUESTIONS TO ASK THE INTERVIEWER — COMPLETE GUIDE
================================================================================

WHY YOUR QUESTIONS MATTER
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
The "Do you have any questions for me?" part of the interview is NOT
formality. It is an active assessment and a genuine opportunity.

INTERVIEWERS USE YOUR QUESTIONS TO ASSESS:
  → Your intellectual curiosity and interest in the role
  → How thoroughly you've done your research
  → How thoughtful and strategic your thinking is
  → What you value in a workplace
  → Whether you're evaluating them as much as they're evaluating you

A candidate who asks great questions stands out. A candidate who says
"I think I'm good, you've covered everything" sends a clear signal: lack
of preparation, lack of curiosity, or disinterest.

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
THE RULES
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
DO:
  ✓ Ask specific, well-researched questions tied to what you've learned
  ✓ Ask questions that give you genuinely useful information
  ✓ Ask 3–5 questions (have more ready; you may not get to all of them)
  ✓ Engage with their answers — follow up naturally
  ✓ Ask different questions to different interviewers (role, team, culture)

DON'T:
  ✗ Ask questions easily answered by the company website or job description
  ✗ Ask about salary, PTO, or benefits (ask recruiter separately)
  ✗ Ask leading questions: "I heard X is a big problem here, is that true?"
  ✗ Ask anything that makes you seem disengaged or not thinking about the role
  ✗ Read questions robotically from a list without listening to prior answers

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
CATEGORY 1 — THE ROLE
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
"What does success look like in this role in the first 90 days?"
  → Gets at concrete expectations; helps you see if they're well-defined

"What are the most important problems this role will be working on in
 the first 6 months?"
  → Lets you evaluate if the work matches what you want to do

"What's the biggest technical challenge the team is currently facing?"
  → Signals you're thinking about hard problems, not just the job

"How much ownership do individual engineers typically have over technical
 decisions at this level?"
  → Clarifies autonomy vs. direction-following culture

"Is this a new role or backfill? What happened to the previous person
 in this role, if it's a backfill?"
  → Honest question that reveals trajectory and retention story

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
CATEGORY 2 — THE TEAM
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
"Can you describe the team composition and how work is typically structured?"
  → Understand if you'll be the only senior, the junior, etc.

"How does the team handle technical debt versus feature development?"
  → Reveals engineering culture: quality-focused vs. always-shipping

"How do you run code reviews and technical design reviews?"
  → Concrete signal of engineering standards

"What's the team's biggest strength? What's the one thing you'd want
 to improve about how the team works together?"
  → Invites honest reflection; shows you think about team dynamics

"How has the team changed in the last year — in size, mission, or composition?"
  → Reveals team stability, growth, and possible organizational volatility

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
CATEGORY 3 — CULTURE & ENGINEERING CRAFT
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
"How do engineers grow here — what does the promotion path look like?"
  → Understand if the company invests in people or just extracts output

"How are decisions made when there's a technical disagreement?"
  → Reveals whether data or hierarchy wins

"How do you approach on-call and incident response?"
  → Signals engineering maturity and respect for work-life balance

"What does 'good engineering' mean to this team or company specifically?"
  → Abstract but valuable — reveals values around craft vs. speed

"How does the engineering team interact with product and design?"
  → Reveals cross-functional health and engineering's seat at the table

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
CATEGORY 4 — THE INTERVIEWER (PERSONAL)
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
These are often the most memorable questions. People love talking about
themselves, and it creates genuine connection.

"What brought you to [Company], and what's kept you here?"
  → Often gets authentic, revealing answers about the culture

"What's the most interesting technical problem you've personally worked
 on since joining?"
  → Reveals their enthusiasm and depth; also gives you intel on the work

"What's one thing you wish you'd known before you joined the team?"
  → Honest and inviting; usually surfaces real cultural insight

"What do you find most challenging about working here?"
  → Invites honesty; signals you want a real picture, not a sales pitch

"What's been your biggest moment of growth at [Company]?"
  → Positive question that reveals learning culture and senior-level honesty

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
CATEGORY 5 — THE COMPANY / PRODUCT DIRECTION
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Use these for hiring managers, directors, or VPs — not for peer interviewers.

"Where do you see the company / product in 2–3 years, and what's the
 engineering investment required to get there?"
  → Shows strategic thinking; tests if they have a clear vision

"What are the biggest bets the team is making technically right now?"
  → Reveals engineering strategy; good signal of forward-thinking

"How does this team's work connect to the company's top priorities?"
  → Reveals alignment — or reveals that the team is disconnected from strategy

"How is the company thinking about [recent news / product change] from
 an engineering perspective?"
  → Shows you've done research; gets you inside intel

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
CATEGORY 6 — EVALUATING THE OFFER (USE LATE-STAGE / AFTER AN OFFER)
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
"What would the typical career trajectory be for someone who excels in
 this role over 2–3 years?"

"Are there other engineering teams I'd interact with regularly, and
 are there lateral move opportunities if my interests evolve?"

"How does the team handle performance feedback — formal reviews,
 continuous feedback, or a mix?"

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
QUESTION SELECTION BY INTERVIEWER TYPE
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
PEER ENGINEER:
  → Tech debt vs. features balance
  → How code reviews work
  → Day-to-day working style
  → Personal growth at the company

HIRING MANAGER:
  → Role expectations + success criteria
  → Team dynamics and structure
  → Career growth and promotion path
  → Biggest challenges facing the team

VP / DIRECTOR (LATE-STAGE):
  → Company direction and engineering strategy
  → Engineering culture and investment in craft
  → Their own career/perspective on the company

RECRUITER:
  → Timeline and next steps
  → Team size and composition
  → General process questions
  → Compensation range (appropriate to ask here)

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
YOUR PRE-INTERVIEW QUESTION LIST TEMPLATE
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Before each interview, prepare 6–8 questions from the above categories,
tailored to the specific role, team, and interviewer.

For each question, note:
  [ ] Question: ___________________________________
  [ ] Why I'm asking: _____________________________
  [ ] What a good vs. red-flag answer looks like: _

Remember: the answers you get are real intelligence for your own evaluation.
You are choosing them as much as they are choosing you.
