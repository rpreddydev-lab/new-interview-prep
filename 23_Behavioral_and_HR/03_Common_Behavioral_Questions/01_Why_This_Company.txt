WHY THIS COMPANY? — COMPLETE GUIDE
================================================================================

WHY THIS QUESTION MATTERS
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
"Why do you want to work here?" is asked in almost every interview.
It is simultaneously one of the easiest and most commonly botched questions.

Companies ask it because they want to hire people who:
  • Genuinely care about the mission, product, or problem space
  • Have done research and thought seriously about the fit
  • Are choosing THIS company, not just any company
  • Are excited about the role, not just desperate for a job

A generic, poorly researched answer signals disinterest — even if you're
technically brilliant. An authentic, specific answer signals that you're
not just interviewing everywhere and hoping something sticks.

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
WHAT A GREAT ANSWER INCLUDES
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
A great "Why this company?" answer has THREE layers:

  LAYER 1: THE PRODUCT / MISSION
    What does the company do that genuinely excites you?
    Be specific to THIS company — not "I want to work at a tech company."

  LAYER 2: THE TECHNICAL CHALLENGE
    What engineering or product problems are they solving that you find
    compelling? The more specific and technical, the more credible.

  LAYER 3: THE PERSONAL FIT
    Why does YOUR background make you particularly well-suited?
    How does this role connect to where you want to go in your career?
    What about the culture/engineering values resonates with you?

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
RESEARCH CHECKLIST — DO THIS 48 HOURS BEFORE THE INTERVIEW
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  □ Company blog / engineering blog (recent posts)
  □ Recent product announcements or launches (last 6–12 months)
  □ Company's stated mission and values
  □ The specific team's work (if publicly available)
  □ The interviewer's background (LinkedIn, talks, publications)
  □ Recent news about the company (funding, acquisitions, direction)
  □ The job description — what problems is the team solving?
  □ Glassdoor / Blind for authentic culture insights
  □ What competitors are doing (gives context for their strategy)

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
WHAT NOT TO SAY
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
✗ "It's a great company with a strong brand."
  → Generic. Everyone can say this. You didn't research.

✗ "I've always wanted to work at a FAANG/top-tier company."
  → This is about your goal, not about them. Signals you'd work anywhere.

✗ "The compensation and benefits are very attractive."
  → True for many — don't lead with this. (Save comp discussion for the offer.)

✗ "I've been using your product for years."
  → Weak on its own without explaining WHY it excites you technically or strategically.

✗ "I heard great things about the culture."
  → Vague. What specifically?

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
FULL EXAMPLE — AMAZON
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
"There are a few things that specifically draw me to Amazon.

 First, the scale. The engineering problems at Amazon's scale — billions
 of requests per day, global infrastructure, real-time personalization at
 100M+ users — are genuinely different from problems at smaller scale. I've
 spent 3 years building payment infrastructure and I want to apply that
 depth to problems I can't currently solve at my current company's scale.

 Second, I've been following AWS's work on [specific service] through your
 engineering blog. The tradeoffs you've written about in distributed
 consistency at scale directly relate to problems I've been wrestling with
 in our settlement system. I want to be in an environment where those
 challenges are the day-to-day work, not the edge cases.

 Third — and I know this sounds clichéd, but I mean it specifically — the
 Leadership Principles resonate with me as an operating model. 'Dive Deep'
 and 'Ownership' in particular are things I try to embody. I want to be
 surrounded by an environment that holds that bar consistently, not just
 values it in a wall poster."

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
FULL EXAMPLE — GOOGLE
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
"I'm drawn to Google for a few specific reasons.

 The problem space: I've been working in ML infrastructure for the past two
 years, and Google's work on TFX and the Vertex AI platform — particularly
 the papers your team has published on large-scale model serving — is exactly
 the kind of technical frontier I want to be contributing to.

 The culture of technical excellence: From everything I've read and from
 conversations with Googlers, there's a genuine respect for engineering
 craft and for people who go deep. My career goal is to become a world-class
 ML systems engineer, and I believe Google is the place where the bar is
 highest and the learning opportunities are most concentrated.

 And specifically this team: I looked at [Team]'s work on [specific recent
 project], and the approach you took to [specific technical decision] was
 fascinating. I'd love to contribute to and learn from work at that level."

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
FULL EXAMPLE — STARTUP (Series B/C)
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
"I've been following [Company] since your Series B announcement. What draws
 me specifically:

 The problem you're solving is genuinely unsolved at scale — [brief
 description of the market gap the company addresses]. I've seen this problem
 firsthand from the vendor side at my current company, so I understand
 both the technical and business dimensions.

 Technically, the challenge of [specific engineering challenge from job
 description or blog] is exactly where I want to be deepening my skills.
 I've done adjacent work in [area] and I'm excited to apply that to your
 specific architecture.

 And the stage matters to me. I joined my current company when it was 40
 people and grew with it to 300. That growth arc taught me more than any
 earlier role I had. I'm deliberately looking for a similar opportunity —
 where I can have real ownership, shape the architecture, and grow with the
 company."

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
PRO TIP: MAKE IT A CONVERSATION
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
After delivering your "Why this company?" answer, end with a question that
invites the interviewer to share their perspective:

  "I'd love to hear what you find most exciting about the problems your
   team is working on right now."

  "What made you join [Company] — and has it met your expectations?"

This turns a one-way interrogation into a genuine conversation, and signals
curiosity, confidence, and genuine interest.
