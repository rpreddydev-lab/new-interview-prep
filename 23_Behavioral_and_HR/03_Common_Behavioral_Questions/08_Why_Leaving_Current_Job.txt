WHY ARE YOU LEAVING YOUR CURRENT JOB? — COMPLETE GUIDE
================================================================================

WHY THIS IS A TRAP QUESTION
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
"Why are you leaving your current job?" (or "Why did you leave your last
 company?") is one of the most common — and most commonly botched —
 interview questions.

It feels like a simple question. It isn't. Interviewers are listening for:

  → Professionalism: Can you discuss your departure without badmouthing?
  → Self-awareness: Do you understand your own motivations?
  → Red flags: Are you fleeing something toxic about you?
  → Fit: Does your reason for leaving make THIS opportunity the right next step?
  → Honesty: Is the stated reason credible and internally consistent?

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
THE CARDINAL RULES
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
RULE 1: NEVER BADMOUTH YOUR CURRENT OR PAST EMPLOYER
  Even if they genuinely deserve it. Even if the story is completely true.
  Interviewers immediately wonder: "Will they talk about us this way too?"
  Negativity is contagious — and disqualifying.

RULE 2: FRAME YOUR DEPARTURE IN TERMS OF WHAT YOU'RE MOVING TOWARD, NOT AWAY FROM
  "I'm excited about X at your company" is always stronger than
  "I'm unhappy with Y at my current company."

RULE 3: BE CONSISTENT
  Your answer must be consistent across all interviewers, the recruiter,
  and anything on your resume or LinkedIn. Inconsistencies = red flags.

RULE 4: BE BRIEF AND FORWARD-LOOKING
  Don't dwell on the past situation. Address the question and pivot quickly
  to what you're excited about in the new role.

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
COMMON REAL REASONS — HOW TO FRAME THEM PROFESSIONALLY
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

REAL REASON: I've hit a ceiling / no room for growth
PROFESSIONAL FRAMING:
  "I've learned a tremendous amount in my current role and I'm proud of what
   the team has accomplished. But I've realized that the next level of growth
   I'm looking for — specifically [domain expertise, scale, leadership scope]
   — isn't available in my current position. This role represents exactly
   that next step."

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
REAL REASON: I don't like my manager
PROFESSIONAL FRAMING:
  "I've had a great run at [Company] — strong team, interesting problems.
   Over time I've realized I work best in an environment with [coaching-based
   leadership / strong engineering culture / direct feedback / technical depth
   from management], and I'm looking for that in my next role."
  NOTE: Do NOT mention the manager specifically. Ever.

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
REAL REASON: The company is struggling / layoffs / instability
PROFESSIONAL FRAMING:
  "The company has been going through a period of strategic reorganization
   that's reduced headcount and shifted priorities. While I understand the
   business context, it's created uncertainty around the projects I care
   most about, and I've decided this is a good time to explore opportunities
   where I can have more consistent impact."
  NOTE: If you were laid off, say so directly — interviewers know how to
  read between the lines and appreciate honesty. "I was part of a
  [5%, 10%] reduction in force in [month/year]" is completely fine.

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
REAL REASON: The work isn't interesting anymore
PROFESSIONAL FRAMING:
  "After [X years], I've delivered the major initiatives I was brought in
   for and the work has become more maintenance-oriented. I'm looking for
   a new challenge — specifically [technical domain, scale, type of problem]
   — and your team's work on [specific area] is genuinely exciting to me."

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
REAL REASON: Compensation is too low
PROFESSIONAL FRAMING:
  Keep compensation out of your answer entirely. It's a legitimate reason
  but not a compelling reason to tell an interviewer — it suggests you're
  purely transactional. Instead, focus on growth and opportunity:
  "I've been focused on the right growth opportunities. When I evaluated
   what I want in my next role, [Company] came up as a place where I can
   [grow in X, work at Y scale, work on Z problems] — and that's what
   excites me about this conversation."
  Compensation can be discussed directly with the recruiter.

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
REAL REASON: I want to switch domains (e.g., enterprise → consumer, or
             infrastructure → product engineering)
PROFESSIONAL FRAMING:
  "I've built deep expertise in [current domain] and I'm really proud of
   what I've contributed. What I've realized is that I want to [work closer
   to the user experience / work on problems that affect millions of people
   directly / work at consumer scale], and that requires a deliberate shift.
   This role represents exactly the domain I want to move into."

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
FULL EXAMPLE ANSWERS
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

EXAMPLE 1 — GROWTH-FOCUSED DEPARTURE:
  "I've been with [Company] for almost 4 years and it's been a fantastic
   experience — I've grown significantly as an engineer and I'm proud of
   the payment infrastructure work I've led there.

   The reason I'm exploring now is that I'm looking for the next level
   of technical challenge. My current role is largely in maintenance and
   optimization mode — the core architecture is stable and the team's focus
   has shifted toward incremental improvements. I want to be working on
   greenfield architecture at a larger scale, and I want to be surrounded
   by the kind of engineering talent that will push me to grow further.

   What drew me specifically to this opportunity is [specific reason] — that's
   exactly the type of problem and scale I'm looking for in my next chapter."

EXAMPLE 2 — LAID OFF:
  "I was part of a 15% reduction in force at [Company] last November.
   It was a company-level decision driven by macro conditions — our team
   specifically had been performing well. It was disappointing, but it's
   given me the opportunity to be intentional about my next move rather
   than reactive.

   I've spent the last 6 weeks being selective — I've turned down a few
   opportunities that weren't the right fit. What drew me to this one is
   [specific reason tied to role, company, or technical problem]."

EXAMPLE 3 — CAREER CHANGE:
  "I've spent 5 years in B2B infrastructure and I've loved the depth of
   engineering challenges there. But I've realized over the past year that
   I want to work on products that millions of consumers use — where the
   feedback loop is faster and the user empathy required is more direct.

   This transition is deliberate: I've been building relevant skills on
   the side, and I've specifically sought out opportunities in consumer
   tech at companies where engineering is central to the product experience.
   This role checks both boxes."

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
HANDLING FOLLOW-UP PROBES
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Common follow-up: "What specifically isn't working in your current role?"
  → Stay professional. Focus on structural/strategic reasons, not people.
  → "The team's direction has shifted toward [type of work] which is less
     aligned with where I want to develop. It's not a bad direction — just
     not the right one for my goals at this stage."

Common follow-up: "Have you considered staying and trying to change it?"
  → Show you've been thoughtful: "I've had honest conversations with my
     manager about the direction and there's genuine alignment that this
     isn't the right growth opportunity for me here. It was a mutual
     recognition, and they've been supportive of my search."

Common follow-up: "What would make you stay?"
  → This is a loyalty test. Answer honestly:
  → "I've made a deliberate decision that this is the right time for a
     change. I'm not looking to be counter-offered back into the status quo."
