WHAT IS A BEHAVIORAL INTERVIEW?
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
A behavioral interview is a structured conversation in which the interviewer
asks you to describe REAL PAST EXPERIENCES to predict your future job
performance. The underlying principle is:

    "Past behavior is the best predictor of future behavior."

Instead of hypothetical ("What would you do if…?"), behavioral questions ask:
    "Tell me about a time when…"
    "Give me an example of…"
    "Describe a situation where…"

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
WHY COMPANIES USE BEHAVIORAL INTERVIEWS
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
1. Reduce bias — structured questions create a level playing field.
2. Assess cultural fit — your stories reveal your values and work style.
3. Verify skills — technical skills can be faked on a resume; stories are harder
   to fabricate consistently.
4. Predict performance — companies like Amazon, Google, and Meta have found
   behavioral signals are highly predictive of on-the-job success.
5. Evaluate soft skills — communication, leadership, collaboration, resilience.

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
HOW BEHAVIORAL INTERVIEWS DIFFER FROM OTHER TYPES
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
┌─────────────────────────┬──────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ Interview Type          │ Focus                                            │
├─────────────────────────┼──────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ Behavioral              │ Past experiences; stories with specific outcomes │
│ Technical / Coding      │ Problem-solving ability; algorithms, systems     │
│ Case                    │ Analytical thinking; structured frameworks       │
│ Situational             │ Hypothetical future scenarios                    │
│ Culture / Values        │ Alignment with company mission and principles    │
└─────────────────────────┴──────────────────────────────────────────────────┘

Most FAANG/big-tech interviews blend technical + behavioral rounds.
Senior+ roles have MORE behavioral emphasis than junior roles.

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
WHAT INTERVIEWERS ARE ACTUALLY EVALUATING
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Every behavioral question is probing one or more of these dimensions:

LEADERSHIP & INFLUENCE
  • Can you drive outcomes without direct authority?
  • Do you mentor, coach, and grow others?
  • Are you proactive vs. reactive?

COLLABORATION & COMMUNICATION
  • Can you work across teams, functions, and time zones?
  • Do you listen, synthesize, and communicate clearly?
  • How do you handle conflict?

PROBLEM-SOLVING & JUDGMENT
  • Do you break down ambiguous problems?
  • Do you make data-driven decisions?
  • Can you balance speed vs. correctness?

OWNERSHIP & ACCOUNTABILITY
  • Do you take initiative without being asked?
  • Do you own outcomes — including failures?
  • Do you follow through?

RESILIENCE & ADAPTABILITY
  • How do you handle failure, setbacks, and feedback?
  • Do you learn and iterate?
  • Can you thrive in uncertainty?

CUSTOMER/USER EMPATHY
  • Do you keep the customer/user at the center?
  • Do you understand impact beyond your immediate task?

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
THE OVERALL STRUCTURE OF A BEHAVIORAL ROUND
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Typical 45–60 minute behavioral interview:

  [0–5 min]   Introductions and small talk
  [5–45 min]  3–5 behavioral questions (deep dives with follow-ups)
  [45–60 min] Your questions to the interviewer

For each question, expect 2–3 follow-up probes such as:
  • "Why did you make that decision?"
  • "What would you do differently?"
  • "Who else was involved and what was their role?"
  • "What was the quantified impact?"

SENIORITY CALIBRATION — WHAT CHANGES AT EACH LEVEL
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
JUNIOR (IC1–IC2 / SWE I–II)
  • Focus: Execution, learning, individual contribution
  • Stories: College projects, internships, small team wins
  • Expected scope: Yourself or your immediate team

MID-LEVEL (IC3 / SWE III)
  • Focus: Reliability, some ownership, technical depth
  • Stories: Owning a feature/service end-to-end
  • Expected scope: Team or cross-team

SENIOR (IC4 / SWE IV, Staff-adjacent)
  • Focus: Influence, ambiguity, driving outcomes
  • Stories: Driving cross-org initiatives, tech strategy, mentoring
  • Expected scope: Organization or multiple teams

STAFF/PRINCIPAL (IC5+)
  • Focus: Org-level impact, long-term vision, culture shaping
  • Stories: Multi-year initiatives, hiring bars, setting direction
  • Expected scope: Company-wide or industry-level

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
HOW STORIES ARE SCORED
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Most companies use a rating scale such as:

  Strong Hire   — Exceptional, clear, quantified, reflective
  Hire          — Good story, covers most dimensions
  No Hire       — Vague, unstructured, no reflection
  Strong No Hire— Poor judgment, blame-shifting, dishonest

Interviewers write detailed notes. Your story must:
  ✓ Be specific (who, what, when, where)
  ✓ Show YOUR role clearly (not "we" without clarity)
  ✓ Quantify impact (numbers, %, time saved, revenue)
  ✓ Demonstrate learning and reflection
  ✓ Be concise (2–3 min for core story, 1 min for follow-ups)

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
THE IDEAL ANSWER ANATOMY
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Use the STAR framework (Situation → Task → Action → Result) — covered in
detail in 01_STAR_Method.txt. The best answers also include:

  REFLECTION / LEARNING
    "If I could do it again, I would…"
    "What I learned was…"
    "This shaped how I approach X today."

  PROACTIVE CONTEXT-SETTING
    "To give you context, our team was responsible for…"
    "The stakes were high because…"

  SPECIFIC, ACTIVE "I" STATEMENTS
    "I proposed…", "I convinced…", "I built…", "I escalated…"

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
PREPARATION CHECKLIST
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
□ Build a "story bank" of 10–15 core stories from your career
□ Tag each story to multiple competencies (one story can serve many questions)
□ Practice out loud — not just in your head
□ Time yourself — core story should be 2–3 minutes
□ Prepare quantified outcomes for every story
□ Research the company's values/frameworks before each interview
□ Prepare 3–5 thoughtful questions to ask at the end
□ Calibrate your story seniority to the role level
