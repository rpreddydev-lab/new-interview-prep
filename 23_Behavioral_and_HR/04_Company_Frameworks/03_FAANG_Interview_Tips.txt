================================================================================
FAANG+ INTERVIEW TIPS — FULL PLAYBOOK
================================================================================

This file covers practical strategy for navigating FAANG and top-tier tech
company interviews: how to research, how to communicate, how to handle
pressure, and how to maximize your offer outcomes.

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
OVERVIEW: WHAT MAKES FAANG INTERVIEWS DIFFERENT
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  1. HIGH BAR: Every interviewer is trained to screen for above-average
     performance. Being "good" doesn't cut it — you need to demonstrate
     excellence on your best days.

  2. STRUCTURED: Behavioral questions are scored on rubrics. Interviewers
     write feedback immediately after each session. Your answer needs to
     land in the moment, not on reflection.

  3. CALIBRATED AGAINST LEVEL: A "Senior Engineer" answer at one company
     may only meet "Mid-level" at Google or Meta. Know the level bar you're
     interviewing for and pitch your stories at that level.

  4. MULTIPLE ROUNDS: Typically 4–6 rounds including: coding, system design,
     and 1–3 behavioral rounds. Behavioral signals carry significant weight.

  5. BAR RAISER / CALIBRATOR: Amazon, Google, and Meta all use a neutral
     calibrator who has a final say on the hire decision beyond the team.

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
PRE-INTERVIEW RESEARCH CHECKLIST
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
For every FAANG interview, complete the following before your first round:

COMPANY RESEARCH:
  □ Read the company's most recent annual report or CEO letter
  □ Follow the company's engineering blog and read 3–5 recent posts
  □ Understand the company's top 3 current strategic bets
  □ Know the company's core product areas and approximate revenue breakdown
  □ Identify a product or technical challenge the company is publicly facing

ROLE RESEARCH:
  □ Read the job description 3 times; identify top 5 core competencies
  □ Look up the team on LinkedIn; understand the team's domain
  □ Research the hiring manager if known (public talks, blog posts)
  □ Understand the team's tech stack and product area

CULTURE RESEARCH:
  □ Read the company's leadership principles, values, or culture deck
  □ Identify 3 ways your professional experience maps to their values
  □ Look up Glassdoor/Levels.fyi/Blind interview reports for that team

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
THE 5-STORY MINIMUM RULE
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Before any FAANG behavioral interview, have at least 5 distinct STAR stories
prepared. Each story should be strong enough to answer multiple questions.

IDEAL STORY PORTFOLIO:
  Story 1: Your biggest technical impact (deep dive / deliver results)
  Story 2: A failure or mistake and what you learned
  Story 3: A conflict or disagreement and how you resolved it
  Story 4: A time you took ownership beyond your scope
  Story 5: A time you demonstrated growth or learned something new

EACH STORY MUST:
  → Use "I" not "we" for the key actions
  → Have a quantified result (%, $, time saved, users impacted)
  → Be recent (last 1–3 years for mid-senior levels)
  → Be detailed enough to handle 3 follow-up questions
  → Take 90–120 seconds to tell before follow-ups

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
HANDLING CURVEBALL AND PRESSURE QUESTIONS
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

WHEN YOU DON'T HAVE A PERFECT STORY:
  → Use the closest story you have and be transparent:
    "I haven't had that exact situation, but the closest experience I have
     is [story]. The principle I'd apply would be..."
  → Never make up a story. Interviewers probe for details and inconsistencies.

WHEN YOU BLANK:
  → Buy 5–10 seconds: "That's a great question. Let me think through
    the best example for this for a moment."
  → Think out loud: "I'm considering two examples — one where I [X] and
    one where I [Y]. I think [Y] is more relevant because..."
  → It's okay to pause. 5 seconds of silence is fine. 30 is not.

WHEN AN INTERVIEWER PUSHES BACK:
  → Don't immediately capitulate. If you believe your answer is right,
    defend it with data or reasoning:
    "I hear your concern about [X]. My thinking was [Y] because of [Z].
     Would it help if I explained the specific tradeoff I was weighing?"
  → If the interviewer is right, acknowledge it genuinely:
    "That's a fair point. In hindsight, [X] would have been a better choice."

WHEN AN INTERVIEWER ASKS HYPOTHETICAL FOLLOW-UPS:
  ("What would you do if the timeline was cut in half?")
  → Treat it as a real question. Walk through your framework:
    "If the timeline was cut in half, the first thing I'd do is..."
  → Show judgment, not just process.

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
SENIORITY CALIBRATION GUIDE
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Interviewers assess WHAT level you're operating at, not just whether the
story is good. Here's how level maps to story scope:

  LEVEL       SCOPE OF STORIES                  INFLUENCE RADIUS
  ─────────   ──────────────────────────────    ─────────────────
  Junior      Feature or task in one project    Self + PR reviews
  Mid         Full project with some x-team     Own team
  Senior      Complex project, x-functional     Multiple teams
  Staff       Org-level initiative              Org / Division
  Principal   Company-level bets                Company / Industry

CALIBRATION MISTAKE: Interviewing for Staff but telling only "I" stories
with no cross-team influence or organizational impact. The interviewer
will downlevel you.

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
REMOTE/VIRTUAL INTERVIEW SETUP
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  → Test your audio, video, and internet 30 minutes before
  → Use a wired connection if possible; have a backup hotspot
  → Headphones with mic — not laptop speaker/mic
  → Clean, well-lit background (plain wall > messy shelf)
  → Have a glass of water nearby; dry mouth is a real issue
  → Close all non-essential browser tabs and applications
  → Have your story notes open — glancing at notes briefly is fine;
    reading from a script is not

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
DAY-OF MENTAL APPROACH
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  → You are evaluating THEM as much as they are evaluating you. Reframe
    the power dynamic — you're choosing your next employer.
  → Interviewers want you to succeed. They're not trying to trap you.
    Their goal is to gather signal, not to make you fail.
  → A 70% answer delivered confidently beats a 90% answer delivered nervously.
  → Every question is a gift: a chance to showcase a story you've prepared.
  → If a round goes badly, reset between rounds. Each round is a clean slate.

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
POST-INTERVIEW PROCESS
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
WITHIN 24 HOURS:
  → Send a brief thank-you email to your recruiter (not the interviewers
    directly, unless specifically invited)
  → Note any questions you struggled with and prepare better answers
  → Do NOT contact interviewers directly or try to add them on LinkedIn

WAITING FOR DECISION:
  → Timeline varies: Amazon (2–5 days), Google (1–4 weeks), Meta (1–2 weeks)
  → Check in with recruiter at the end of the stated timeline, not before
  → Polite check-in: "Hi [name], I wanted to follow up as I'm still very
    excited about the opportunity. I know the team takes time to debrief —
    is there an updated timeline you can share?"

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
COMPANY-SPECIFIC QUICK REFERENCE
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
AMAZON:
  → All behavioral questions map to the 16 Leadership Principles
  → Use "I" not "we" — Amazon probes hard for individual contribution
  → Quantify everything: $, %, customers, time
  → Expect a Bar Raiser who will ask the hardest questions
  → Study: 00_Amazon_Leadership_Principles.txt

GOOGLE:
  → 4 attributes: Cognitive Ability, Leadership, Googleyness, Role Knowledge
  → "Emergent leadership" — no formal authority required
  → Intellectual humility + curiosity are heavily weighted
  → Expect: "What would you do differently?"
  → Study: 01_Google_Googleyness_and_Leadership.txt

META:
  → "Jedi interviews" — Judgment, Ethics, Decisiveness, Influence
  → They calibrate by scope and scale (users, teams impacted, $ value)
  → Speed + iteration mindset — "move fast and learn"
  → Cross-team collaboration stories score very high
  → Study: 02_Meta_Core_Values.txt

MICROSOFT:
  → Growth mindset is central — failure + learning stories are crucial
  → "Model, Coach, Care" is the manager framework; IC = "Collaborate, Create"
  → Customer focus, partnering, and diversity are assessed
  → Questions often include: "Tell me about a time you helped someone else
    succeed" and "Tell me about a time you learned from failure"

APPLE:
  → Craft and quality are paramount — "best we can do" culture
  → Collaboration within secrecy: how do you work across teams on a product
    that can't be discussed publicly?
  → Direct, confident communication — Apple interviewers expect conviction
  → Key value: "You don't do it right until it's right"

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
FINAL CHECKLIST — 48 HOURS BEFORE YOUR INTERVIEW
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  □ Review company's leadership principles / values
  □ Re-read job description — identify top 3 competencies
  □ Review your 5 core stories — practice out loud
  □ Prepare your "Tell Me About Yourself" (2-minute version)
  □ Prepare 3 questions to ask the interviewer
  □ Confirm logistics (time, format, link/location)
  □ Prepare your physical/virtual setup
  □ Sleep 7+ hours the night before
