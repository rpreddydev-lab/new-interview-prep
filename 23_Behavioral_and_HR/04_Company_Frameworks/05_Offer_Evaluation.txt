================================================================================
OFFER EVALUATION — HOW TO ANALYZE AND COMPARE JOB OFFERS
================================================================================

WHY OFFER EVALUATION IS A SKILL
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Most engineers evaluate offers by looking at total compensation and picking
the highest number. This leads to regret. The best career decisions account
for compensation AND trajectory, culture fit, learning curve, and personal
circumstances.

This guide gives you a structured framework for evaluating any offer —
especially when comparing multiple at once.

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
PART 1: TOTAL COMPENSATION ANALYSIS
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Before comparing offers, normalize everything to ANNUALIZED TOTAL COMPENSATION.

ANNUALIZED TC FORMULA:
  Annualized TC = Base + (Equity Grant / Vest Years) + Expected Bonus
               + (Signing Bonus / Likely Tenure Years)

EXAMPLE:
  Offer A:
    Base: $180,000
    RSU grant: $400,000 over 4 years = $100,000/yr
    Target bonus: 10% = $18,000
    Signing: $30,000 (likely stay 2 years) = $15,000/yr
    Annualized TC ≈ $313,000

  Offer B:
    Base: $200,000
    RSU grant: $200,000 over 4 years = $50,000/yr
    Target bonus: 15% = $30,000
    Signing: $50,000 (likely stay 2 years) = $25,000/yr
    Annualized TC ≈ $305,000

Even though Offer B has a higher base, Offer A has higher total comp.
Without this analysis, most candidates would instinctively favor Offer B.

ADDITIONAL MONETARY FACTORS:
  → 401k match: 3–6% of base is worth $5K–$12K/yr at this salary range
  → Health insurance quality: premium differences can be $3K–$10K/yr
  → Remote flexibility: saves commute cost and time
  → Relocation package: if required, factor in cost of living change
  → ESPP (Employee Stock Purchase Plan): free money, often 10–15% discount

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
PART 2: CAREER TRAJECTORY ANALYSIS
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
The job that pays the most today may not be the best long-term career choice.
A job that pays 10% less but gives you a better title, faster promotion path,
or higher-quality skills may be worth $200K more in year 5.

QUESTIONS TO ASSESS TRAJECTORY:

  THE TEAM:
  → Will I be the strongest or weakest engineer on this team?
    (Weakest = faster growth; strongest = more responsibility, less growth)
  → Who will be my day-to-day technical mentors?
  → What is the team's reputation in the company?

  THE MANAGER:
  → Does this manager have a track record of developing their reports?
  → What's the attrition rate on their team?
  → Do they advocate for their team's visibility and promotion?
  → Ask: "How many people have you promoted in the last 2 years?"

  THE SCOPE:
  → What will I be responsible for in 6 months? In 18 months?
  → Is the scope expanding or contracting?
  → Will this role give me new skills I don't currently have?

  PROMOTION PATH:
  → What is the typical time to promotion at this level?
  → What does the next-level performance bar look like?
  → Ask: "What would I need to demonstrate in the first 12 months to be
    on track for promotion?"

  BRAND AND MARKET VALUE:
  → Will this company name open doors at my next job?
  → Is the team or product domain marketable?
  → FAANG experience compounds — a 2-year stint at Google/Meta pays
    dividends for 10+ years in the job market

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
PART 3: CULTURE AND DAY-TO-DAY FIT
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
A high-paying job in a bad culture will make you miserable. Culture is harder
to assess than compensation but equally important.

CULTURE SIGNALS TO GATHER:
  → How did you feel after every interview? Energized or drained?
  → Did interviewers seem engaged, genuine, and respectful?
  → What did Glassdoor/Blind reviewers say about the team specifically?
  → What did you learn about how decisions are made?
    (Top-down vs. bottom-up, fast vs. process-heavy)

ASK THESE QUESTIONS IN INTERVIEWS:
  "What does success look like in this role after 6 months?"
  "How does the team make technical decisions?"
  "What's one thing you would change about the team's culture?"
  "What do the strongest engineers on this team have in common?"

WATCH FOR RED FLAGS:
  ✗ Interviewers couldn't clearly describe the team's mission
  ✗ High attrition mentioned casually ("people come and go here")
  ✗ Negative comments about previous people in the role
  ✗ Vague or deflective answers to culture questions
  ✗ "We work hard" as the primary selling point
  ✗ All-male interview panel with no diversity in sight

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
PART 4: LIFE FIT ANALYSIS
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Your career exists inside your life. The best professional choice and the
best personal choice are not always the same thing.

FACTORS TO WEIGH:
  → LOCATION: Is it remote, hybrid, or in-office?
    How does commute/relocation affect your life quality?
  → HOURS/PACE: Is this a "ship fast, always on" culture or a "sustainable
    pace" culture? Which do you want right now?
  → STABILITY: Is this a public company with predictable equity or a startup
    where the equity may never vest?
  → TEAM DYNAMICS: Will I be energized by these people or depleted?
  → MISSION ALIGNMENT: Do I care about what this company does?
    Mission alignment reduces attrition, increases engagement.

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
DECIDING AND DECLINING
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

WHEN TO ACCEPT:
  → You've completed the scorecard and one offer is clearly better
  → You've negotiated and are satisfied
  → You're not waiting on a more preferred offer that will arrive in time
  → Your walk-away date has arrived (don't keep employers waiting > 5–7 days)

HOW TO DECLINE PROFESSIONALLY:
  "Hi [Recruiter Name], I wanted to reach out personally to let you know
   that after a lot of consideration, I've decided to accept an offer with
   another company. This was genuinely a difficult decision — I have a lot
   of respect for [Company] and the team I met. I hope our paths cross
   again in the future."

  RULES:
  → Decline quickly — don't ghost or go silent
  → Be warm, brief, and final — don't explain at length
  → Leave the door open — tech is small; people remember
  → Never burn a bridge by negotiating after you've decided

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
RENEGING ON AN ACCEPTED OFFER (LAST RESORT GUIDANCE)
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Once you've accepted an offer, reneging is a serious professional act.
It damages your reputation and the recruiter's. Do it only in extreme cases
(personal emergency, company's material circumstances changed, etc.)

IF YOU MUST RENEGE:
  → Call the recruiter directly (don't email as the first communication)
  → Be honest and brief — don't invent reasons
  → Apologize genuinely without over-explaining
  → Accept the relationship damage gracefully
