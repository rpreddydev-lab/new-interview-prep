================================================================================
SALARY NEGOTIATION — COMPLETE GUIDE FOR ENGINEERS
================================================================================

THE FUNDAMENTAL RULE
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Every offer is negotiable. Companies expect negotiation and budget for it.
The first offer is almost never the best offer.

The candidate who negotiates professionally and confidently:
  → Almost never loses the offer
  → Almost always gets more
  → Signals they are a professional who understands their value

The candidate who doesn't negotiate:
  → Leaves money on the table (often $10K–$50K+ in year one)
  → Sets a lower base for all future raises and offers at other companies

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
KNOW YOUR NUMBERS BEFORE YOU NEGOTIATE
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

RESEARCH TOOLS:
  → levels.fyi         — Most accurate TC data for FAANG/big tech
  → glassdoor.com      — Broad market data, less precise
  → linkedin.com/salary — Good for mid-market and non-tech
  → blind              — Anonymous self-reports, useful for culture intel
  → teamblind.com      — Similar to Blind
  → paysa.com          — Personalized salary estimates
  → H1B salary databases — Actual disclosed salaries for visa holders

KNOW THESE 5 NUMBERS BEFORE ANY NEGOTIATION:
  1. Market rate for this role/level at this company
  2. Your current total compensation (broken down by component)
  3. Your target total compensation (your "happy" number)
  4. Your walk-away number (the minimum you'll accept)
  5. Other competing offers (real or in progress)

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
TOTAL COMPENSATION COMPONENTS — KNOW WHAT TO NEGOTIATE
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Most tech offers include multiple components — each can be negotiated:

  BASE SALARY
  Most stable component. Forms the basis of all future raises.
  Negotiate this first and highest — it compounds.

  EQUITY (RSUs / STOCK OPTIONS)
  At FAANG/big tech: RSUs vest over 4 years (typically 25%/yr or front-loaded)
  Negotiate: Total grant value AND vesting cliff AND refresh grants
  Watch out for: Underwater options at pre-IPO companies

  SIGNING BONUS
  One-time payment, often used to offset unvested equity you're leaving.
  Negotiable — ask for it even if not offered.
  Clawback: Most signing bonuses have a clawback clause if you leave <1–2 yrs.

  ANNUAL BONUS / PERFORMANCE BONUS
  Variable, often tied to company + individual performance
  Less predictable. Focus negotiation on base + equity first.

  BENEFITS
  Health, dental, vision, 401k match, PTO, remote work policy, relocation.
  Benefits have dollar value — include them in your total comp calculation.

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
NEGOTIATION TIMING PRINCIPLES
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

RULE 1: DO NOT GIVE A NUMBER FIRST
Whoever gives a number first anchors the negotiation. Deflect:
  "I'd like to learn more about the full package and role before discussing
   compensation expectations."
  "I'm flexible and open to discussing that — could you share what the
   budget for this role looks like?"

RULE 2: DELAY UNTIL YOU HAVE AN OFFER
Never negotiate in early interviews. Bring it up only when:
  (a) They ask you directly what you're looking for, OR
  (b) You have a formal written offer in hand

RULE 3: ALWAYS NEGOTIATE IN WRITING (OR BE WILLING TO CONFIRM IN WRITING)
Verbal agreements are forgettable. After any verbal negotiation:
  "Thanks so much — could you put that in writing so I can review the full
   updated package?"

RULE 4: USE COMPETING OFFERS AS LEVERAGE — EVEN INFORMALLY
  "I'm currently in final rounds with another company and expect to have
   their offer within the week. I'm very excited about this role and would
   love to make this work — is there flexibility on the base?"

You do not need to reveal the other company or exact number to create urgency.

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
SALARY NEGOTIATION SCRIPTS
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

WHEN ASKED: "What are your salary expectations?"
  VERSION 1 (deflect):
  "I'm still learning about the scope of the role and would prefer to
   discuss once we both know there's a mutual fit. What's the budgeted
   range for this position?"

  VERSION 2 (if forced to give a number):
  "Based on my research on levels.fyi and conversations in the market,
   I've seen compensation for this role at this level range from [X] to [Y].
   I'd expect to be in the upper part of that range given my [specific
   experience or skills]. But I'm open to discussing the full package."

WHEN YOU RECEIVE AN OFFER:
  DO NOT accept or reject immediately. Say:
  "Thank you so much — I'm genuinely excited about this opportunity. I'd
   like to take a day or two to review the full package carefully. When
   do you need a decision by?"

WHEN COUNTER-OFFERING:
  "Thank you for the offer — I'm very excited about the role and the team.
   After reviewing everything, I was hoping we could get the base salary
   to [TARGET]. Based on my research and my [X years of experience in Y],
   I believe that reflects my market value accurately."

  Then: STOP TALKING. Silence after your counteroffer is powerful.

WHEN THEY PUSH BACK:
  "I understand there may be constraints. Would there be flexibility in
   other parts of the package — equity or signing bonus — that could help
   close the gap?"

WHEN THEY SAY "THIS IS OUR BEST OFFER":
  "I appreciate that. Can I ask — is the equity grant or signing bonus
   also at the max, or is there more flexibility there?"
  (Often base is capped but equity/signing are not.)

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
SPECIFIC NEGOTIATION SCENARIOS
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

SCENARIO 1: NO COMPETING OFFER
Frame around market data:
  "My research on levels.fyi shows the 75th percentile for this role and
   level is $X. I'd love to align closer to that figure."

SCENARIO 2: COMPETING OFFER
  "I have an offer from [company, or 'another company in the same space']
   that's coming in higher. I'm genuinely more excited about this role,
   but the gap is meaningful — is there anything you can do?"

SCENARIO 3: LEAVING UNVESTED EQUITY
  "I have approximately $X of unvested equity that I'd be walking away from.
   A signing bonus that covers a portion of that would make this decision
   much easier."

SCENARIO 4: PRE-IPO COMPANY (OPTIONS vs. RSUs)
  → Get the strike price, current 409A valuation, and last preferred round valuation
  → Calculate best-case dilution scenarios
  → Ask: "What percentage of the company does my grant represent?"
  → Consider: can you see their cap table? Are there liquidation preferences?

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
WHAT NOT TO DO
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  ✗ Accept the first offer without negotiating
  ✗ Give a number first ("I'm looking for X")
  ✗ Negotiate via emotional appeal ("I really need the money")
  ✗ Be aggressive or ultimatum-based ("That's unacceptable, I need X or I walk")
  ✗ Lie about competing offers — if you say you have an offer, they may ask
    to see it, and being caught in a lie ends the negotiation
  ✗ Take more than 1 week to make a decision (tests relationship goodwill)
  ✗ Negotiate after accepting — once you accept, you're done
